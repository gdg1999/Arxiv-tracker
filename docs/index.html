Abstract
Spline representations provide a compact way to approximate continuous trajectories using a finite number of parameters. When approximating trajectories generated by dynamical systems, the efficiency of a spline representation depends strongly on the placement of knot points. Uniform knot distributions often lead to inefficient representations, requiring either unnecessary control points or producing poor approximations in regions where the trajectory varies rapidly. In this work we propose a learning-based method for adaptive knot placement in B-spline approximations of trajectories generated by ordinary differential equations. A neural network learns a mapping from the initial condition of the system to quantities that characterize the local variation of the resulting trajectory. These estimates are used to construct nonuniform knot distributions that approximately equidistribute a local error indicator, reducing the number of control points needed to meet a prescribed accuracy.

Report on Stability Analysis of Islanded Power Systems with Grid-Forming Converters

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Islanded power systems are inherently challenging to operate due to limited interconnection, low inertia, and weak short-circuit strength. As renewable and inverter-based resources replace synchronous machines, these challenges worsen, causing faster frequency deviations, greater voltage volatility, and higher risk of instability. This report summarizes the differences between islanded and interconnected systems and reviews key stability issues in islanded grids: frequency stability, converter-driven interactions, voltage stability, large-disturbance behavior, and small-signal dynamics. Grid-forming (GFM) voltage source converters are a promising solution because they can establish voltage and frequency, emulate synchronous machine behavior, and provide grid strength, virtual inertia, fast frequency response, voltage regulation, and black-start capability. The report examines relevant GFM control strategies and evaluates their capabilities in weak, low-inertia islanded grids. Challenges remain, particularly interoperability, control coordination among multiple inverter-based resources, lack of standardized grid code requirements, and coordination of protection devices. Successful deployment requires advances in interoperability, harmonized grid codes, and grid planning that considers the unique features of islanded systems, along with clear GFM functionality requirements. Finally, a case study illustrates the stability-enhancing capability of GFM converters in weak grids through small-signal stability analysis. Overall, GFM technology is a key enabler of reliable, renewable-dominant island power systems, but broader adoption depends on continued technical and regulatory development.

Koopman Greedy Spiking Control for Nonlinear Systems under Firing-Rate Limits
Authors: Nader Meskin
First: 2026-10-30T10:23:32+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-30T10:23:32+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Neuromorphic controllers interact with physical systems through fixed-amplitude spikes whose timing conveys control information. Existing Lyapunov-based greedy spiking controllers guarantee stability but are restricted to linear systems and neglect firing-rate limits. We extend greedy spiking control to nonlinear systems using Koopman operator theory. For observable dictionaries invariant under differentiation along the input direction, spike-induced state translations admit an exact linear representation in lifted coordinates, enabling exact modeling of spiking jump dynamics. This allows firing decisions to be formulated as comparisons of quadratic Lyapunov functions in the lifted space. We prove practical stability of a clocked, rate-limited controller with an explicit ultimate bound and develop a data-driven design based on Koopman eigenfunctions learned from unforced data by generator-based regression. The weights of the Lyapunov function are synthesized by a linear program that enforces a sampled relaxation of the rate-limited control-Lyapunov condition. Applied to an inverted pendulum, the learned eigenfunctions recover the system energy, and the proposed controller achieves swing-up and stabilization from nearly all initial conditions despite severe actuation limits.
Summary / 总结
Neuromorphic controllers interact with physical systems through fixed-amplitude spikes whose timing conveys control information.
Riemannian Optimization on Unknown Manifolds via Stochastic Proximal Refinement
Authors: Ruixiang Wu, Jiahao Ai, Tinko Sebastian Bartels, Xuanzhuo Liu, Yifei Wu, Yu Mao, Tongxin Li, Costas Courcoubetis
First: 2026-10-30T07:44:33+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-30T07:44:33+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We study Riemannian optimization on an unknown manifold accessed only through feasible samples. The central challenge is to turn feasible samples into optimization steps, i.e., the samples are drawn from the unknown feasible manifold, but do not directly provide the tangent spaces, projections, or retractions required by classic Riemannian methods. Moreover, regions with low objective values may be sparsely represented in the data, so directly sampling from a distribution fitted to these observations can require many draws to find better feasible solutions. We introduce Proximal Refinement on Implicit Smooth Manifolds (PRISM), which turns feasible-sample access into an iterative optimization procedure by repeatedly sampling an objective-aware proximal distribution conditioned on the current iterate. Theoretically, we show that PRISM recovers the effect of Riemannian-gradient refinement, leading to provable objective descent, feasibility (manifold adherence), and convergence toward first-order stationarity. We demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach through synthetic experiments illustrating its geometric properties and applications to data-driven building control and diverse real world optimization tasks, highlighting its potential as a general framework for data-driven optimization across scientific and engineering domains.

We study Riemannian optimization on an unknown manifold accessed only through feasible samples.
Multi-Timescale Deliverability of Shared Hybrid Storage for AI Data Center Campuses
Authors: Zihan Meng, Ruizhang Yang, Chenxi Hu, Francis Yunhe Hou
First: 2026-10-30T04:48:43+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-30T04:48:43+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Artificial intelligence data center (AIDC) campuses contain sustained power variations and subsecond power fluctuations, requiring both sufficient stored energy and fast response. A shared hybrid energy storage system (HESS) can mitigate aggregate AIDC power deviations, but interval-based schedules cannot capture the delay between commanded and delivered power at the campus point of common coupling (PCC). To this end, this paper proposes a multi-timescale scheduling model for mitigating AIDC power fluctuations with the shared HESS. First, it models electrical pooling at the campus bus and constrains the shared HESS to a single PCC injection. Second, a two-layer model passes five-minute scheduled power, energy state, and reserve capacity to a causal fast response model that distinguishes commanded power from delivered power. Case studies using the National Laboratory of the Rockies (NLR) GenAI Power Profiles Dataset show reductions in both PCC and fast integral absolute error (IAE), while retaining most of the improvement available under ideal response.

Control-Layer Interpretation of Reactive Power Recovery Limits in EMT-RMS Model Benchmarking of Inverter-Based Resources
Authors: Dileep Cheerla, Amirthagunaraj Yogarathnam, Divya Jain, Satish Natti
First: 2026-10-30T03:59:22+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-30T03:59:22+00:00
Comments: Accepted for presentation at IEEE PES Grid Edge Technologies 2027, April 19-22, 2027, Salt Lake City, Utah, USA.5 pages, 3 figures, 6 tables
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
EMT--RMS benchmarking is widely used to verify cross-platform consistency of inverter-based resource (IBR) dynamic models. This paper examines a 200 MW anonymized IBR represented in PSCAD and PSS/E. The EMT model separates inverter electrical capability from a plant-level power plant controller (PPC) recovery target, whereas the RMS model represents these functions through REECC and REPCA parameters. Directly assigning the PPC target of $\pm 0.2764$ pu to REPCA hard reactive limits is initialization-inconsistent at the original lagging operating point because the initial reactive command is 0.396273 pu. A second, near-unity-power-factor operating point initializes successfully with the same diagnostic limit, demonstrating that the conflict is operating-point dependent. Using the non-conflicting capability mapping removes the original hard-limit conflict, but the benchmark still shows 18.5--30.2 MVAr post-event POI reactive-power differences from PSCAD. A one-at-a-time REPCA $K_c$ sensitivity under matched benchmark initial conditions further shows that RMS recovery is parameter- and limit-dependent: at $K_c=0.05$ and 0.10 the aggregate machine reactive output reaches the configured capability bounds, while $K_c=0.15$ moves VDOWN, VUP, and HVRT off those bounds and changes their settled POI reactive power by 8.94, 18.00, and 3.17 MVAr, respectively. Machine-to-POI accounting further shows why machine-side saturation need not reproduce the POI-level PPC recovery magnitude. The results distinguish initialization feasibility from recovery equivalence and provide a control-layer diagnostic workflow beyond waveform comparison.

EMT--RMS benchmarking is widely used to verify cross-platform consistency of inverter-based resource (IBR) dynamic models.
A Closed-loop Framework to Discriminate Models Using Optimal Control
Authors: Laurent Pagnier, Melvyn Tyloo, Akshita Jindal, Pragati Thakur, Kyle C. A. Wedgwood
First: 2026-02-28T15:32:26+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-29T21:56:16+00:00

Abstract
Predicting the response of an observed system to a known input is a fruitful first step to accurately control the system's dynamics. Despite the recent advances in fully data-driven algorithms, the most interpretable way to reach this goal is through mechanistic mathematical modeling. Here, we leverage optimal control and propose a closed-loop iterative method to choose among a set of candidate models the one that most accurately predicts an observed system. We assume that one has control over an input of the observed system and access to measurements of its response. Our approach is to identify the input control that maximally discriminates the response of the candidate models, allowing us to determine which model is best by comparing such responses with the observed data. We demonstrate our proposed framework in numerical simulations before applying it during an electrophysiology experiment, successfully discriminating between different models for photocurrents produced via opsin dynamics.

RC Prediction: A Python Package for Parameter-Aware Reservoir Computing
Authors: Jin-Cheng Rao, Zheng-Meng Zhai, Ying-Cheng Lai
First: 2026-10-29T21:08:49+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-29T21:08:49+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
A nonlinear dynamical system can be characterized by the autonomous evolution of its state in time and its dependence on system parameters. As a parameter varies, the dynamics can change quantitatively and, at bifurcation points, qualitatively. When the governing equations are unknown, a data-driven model should therefore capture both elements from observational data, rather than reproduce the dynamics at a single operating condition only. Parameter-aware reservoir computing (PARC) is a model-free framework for this purpose, where the parameter is supplied to a reservoir computer through an additional input channel. PARC is trained on time series recorded at several parameter values, and the trained reservoir, run in closed loop as an autonomous dynamical system, generates trajectories at parameter values that were never observed. rc_prediction is an open-source Python package that implements this workflow. It provides multi-parameter training of a reservoir, autonomous closed-loop prediction at user-specified parameter values, parameter scans, and a standard echo state network for forecasting at a fixed parameter. Hyperparameter optimization and optional GPU acceleration through PyTorch are also provided. We use three benchmark dynamical systems as examples to demonstrate its usage. As one of the featured applications, here we illustrate the prediction of critical transitions and system collapse: trained only on pre-critical data, the model is queried beyond the training range to predict whether the system collapses, to estimate the critical parameter $p_c^{*}$, and to characterize transient lifetimes.
Summary / 总结
A nonlinear dynamical system can be characterized by the autonomous evolution of its state in time and its dependence on system parameters.
Learning the Omega-Limit Partition from Approximate Koopman Invariants
Authors: Dhruv Shah, Jorge Cortés
First: 2026-10-29T21:01:32+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-29T21:01:32+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Nonlinear dynamical systems with multiple omega-limit sets pose a fundamental challenge for global finite-dimensional Koopman modeling with continuous observables. A natural way to address this challenge is to decompose the state space into forward-invariant regions associated with distinct asymptotic behaviors and to learn a separate Koopman model for each region. This paper studies how to compute such partitions from data. We establish conditions under which Koopman-invariant observables, namely eigenfunctions associated with the unit eigenvalue, characterize the stationary partition induced by omega-limit sets. Building on this characterization, we develop OPAL (Omega-limit Partitions from Approximate invariants Learned from data), an algorithm that extracts partitions from approximate invariant observables. Numerical examples show that the recovered partitions capture the relevant asymptotic regions and support multiple regional linear Koopman models with significantly improved long-horizon prediction capabilities.

Temporal and spatial couplings in the dynamics of human walking and running
Authors: Mustafa Mert Ankarali
First: 2026-10-29T20:25:14+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-29T20:25:14+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Rhythmic behaviours such as walking and running are commonly modelled as dynamical systems around a stable limit cycle. To keep such models tractable, it is usually assumed that each step depends only on the previous one, that the limit cycle is fixed, and that the sagittal and frontal planes can be studied separately. Here, we test these assumptions in seven public datasets of human gait (71 participants), including 16 people who both walked and ran. We fit linear step-to-step maps and validate them by cross-validation, holding out more data where more data exist. At first sight, gait has memory: the state two steps back lowers the prediction error by up to 29\%. Yet most of this temporal coupling vanishes once the operating point may drift slowly and measurement noise is modelled; gait remains limit-cycle-like, around an operating point that moves. Running keeps more memory than walking in the same people. Spatial coupling between the planes is present in most participants, mainly from sagittal to frontal, but weak. This supports planar models of healthy gait, while a repeatable, task-dependent coupling index may serve as a measure of gait quality. The simple models are wrong, but useful enough.

Rhythmic behaviours such as walking and running are commonly modelled as dynamical systems around a stable limit cycle.
