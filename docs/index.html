Foundation Models Meet Power Grid Topology for Cascade Prediction
Authors: Soumav Biswas, Mia Naeini
First: 2026-10-23T14:21:47+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-23T14:21:47+00:00
Comments: 10 pages
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Accurate early-stage prediction of cascading failure severity is essential for assessing the vulnerability of power transmission networks. However, existing data-driven approaches often rely on network-specific representations, limiting their generalizability across power system topologies. This work investigates the complementary capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) and Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) for cascading failure severity prediction. First, standalone LLM predictors are developed using a LoRA-fine-tuned LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct model with varying degrees of topological information. A hybrid Graph-LLM fusion framework is then proposed, integrating a Topology-Aware Graph Attention Transformer (TAGAT) with the LLM through confidence-gated fusion. TAGAT captures structural dependencies among transmission lines, while the LLM extracts representations from early-stage power-flow observations and aggregated cascade descriptors. The frameworks are evaluated on 30,000 simulated cascading failure scenarios for each of the IEEE 118-bus and IEEE 300-bus systems. Using only the first four cascade steps, the hybrid framework achieves weighted F1 scores of 0.94 and 0.92, respectively. In cross-system transfer from IEEE 118-bus to IEEE 300-bus, it achieves 0.72 zero-shot accuracy, improving to 0.83 with limited target-system calibration, outperforming standalone models and conventional baselines. These results show the potential of combining graph-based structural learning with foundation models to improve cascading failure severity prediction and cross-system transferability.

Iterative Behavioral Identification for Covert Misappropriation Attacks
Authors: Victor Mattos, Amit Bhaya, Daniel Sadoc Menasche, Alan O. de Sá
First: 2026-10-23T14:06:32+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-23T14:06:32+00:00
Comments: Presented at the 17th APCA International Conference on Automatic Control and Soft Computing (CONTROLO 2026), Coimbra, Portugal, September 9-11, 2026
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We present a data-driven, three-phase framework for covert misappropriation attacks on closed-loop networked control systems. A man-in-the-middle (MITM) adversary, with access only to intercepted input-output data, incrementally identifies the plant's behavioral subspace while maintaining stealth under an infinity-norm intrusion-detection system (IDS). Phase 1 collects an initial rank-deficient Hankel matrix using a low-amplitude probing signal that satisfies an analytical stealth bound. Phase 2 adapts online rank-increasing experiment design to the closed-loop setting, using causal predictor-based masking to limit IDS residuals while the Hankel rank grows from 1 to the full behavioral rank n + mL in exactly n + mL - 1 steps. We show that the identified subspaces are strictly nested and that the projection error of any desired trajectory decreases monotonically, strictly so almost surely at each rank increase. Phase 3 employs the final Hankel matrix in a DeePC-style optimization to synthesize a covert attack that tracks an arbitrary reference while respecting the IDS constraint. Simulations on a DC motor with PI control demonstrate full-rank identification and stealthy tracking. Stealth is guaranteed analytically in Phases 1 and 3 and is numerically supported in Phase 2. The method is model-free and assumes noise-free LTI dynamics.

We present a data-driven, three-phase framework for covert misappropriation attacks on closed-loop networked control systems.
Controller-Agnostic Runtime Safety Certificates for Tool-Using AI Agents via Data-Driven Robust Reachability
Authors: Ali Baheri, Peng Wei
First: 2026-10-12T14:30:31+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-23T10:58:05+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Software agents built on large language models act by issuing tool calls, some of which cannot be undone. To control theory the model is an opaque feedback controller, replaced every few months, whose closed loop cannot be analyzed directly. We propose a runtime filter, or shield, between any such controller and its environment that certifies a bound on the probability of reaching an unsafe condition during a bounded task. Given a predicate abstraction and candidate successor sets, everything else comes from logs: the loop becomes a finite controlled Markov chain, the estimated kernel is surrounded by a distribution-free confidence region, and robust reach-avoid value iteration runs over it. The certificate is probably approximately correct and uniform over controllers respecting the filter, so one model may replace another without recomputation. In all tested datasets the true risk stayed within certificate and budget; in a file-system sandbox the filter stays sound against a hazard the logs barely covered, where a plug-in baseline certifies safety and fails; and it holds seven controllers, three of them open-weights models, to zero unsafe outcomes in 1050 episodes.

Software agents built on large language models act by issuing tool calls, some of which cannot be undone.
Model-Free Output-Admittance Reshaping for Stability Enhancement of Grid-Connected Inverters
Authors: Runnan Wang, Wanrong Li, Huawei Yuan, Sinan Li
First: 2026-10-23T02:56:44+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-23T02:56:44+00:00
Comments: Accepted at the 2026 IEEE PES Australasian Universities Power Engineering Conference (AUPEC)
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Grid-connected inverters can be unstable when their current-control loops interact with an uncertain external network. Returning the existing controller to improve damping also alters current tracking because tracking dynamics and output admittance are coupled. This paper adds a local model-free control (MFC) loop around the existing current controller to reduce output admittance while closely preserving nominal current-tracking dynamics. The added loop uses local inverter parameters without an external grid or neighbouring converter model. A Norton formulation separates reference tracking from the current response to terminal-voltage disturbances and establishes a tracking-preservation condition for admittance reshaping. Reducing the disturbance response makes the inverter behave more like an ideal controlled current source. Averaged-model simulations show reductions exceeding 65% in the magnitudes of all four dq output-admittance elements over 10-200 Hz, with closely preserved current-tracking dynamics. Generalised Nyquist analysis predicts small-signal stability for all sampled grid-impedance cases with MFC, compared with 90.0% without MFC.
Summary / 总结
Grid-connected inverters can be unstable when their current-control loops interact with an uncertain external network.
The BAR-SOT Method: Long-term Average Cost Control as Stochastic Optimal Self-Transport
Authors: Sharan Srinivasan, Berke M. Turkay, Harsha Honnappa
First: 2026-09-16T00:49:41+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-23T01:10:22+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We reformulate average-cost (ergodic) control of a Markov jump process as a finite-horizon stochastic optimal transport (SOT) problem that jointly optimizes over the controlled evolution and the marginal law from which it starts and returns to (i.e., a self-transport). The constraint relating the marginal flow to the controlled generator is the basic adjoint relationship (BAR), so we call the resulting problem BAR-SOT. For any time horizon T>0, its optimal value is a constant scaling of the long-run average-cost rate. We give three equivalent formulations (through controlled processes, a Fokker--Planck constraint, and relaxed marginal measures) and show that the optimal dual is a stationary potential plus a term linear in time, with slope equal to the rate. A relative-entropy penalty on the control yields a cost-tilted Schrodinger bridge problem, computable by a Sinkhorn-type iteration when every transition rate is controlled, whose value converges to the unregularized optimum as the penalty vanishes. We develop the theory for finite Markov decision processes and then for general controlled Markov jump processes. A neural parametrization of the dual, trained as a physics-informed neural network (PINN), that encodes Harrison's equivalent-workload formulation (see Harrison 2000) matches the strong reinforcement-learning baseline of Dai and Gluzman (2022); numerically conditioning the fit of a sub-dominant transverse correction then improves on both that baseline and the best priority heuristic. On the input-queued switch, a graph-attention parametrization of the dual improves on the strongest matching heuristic we are aware of, with a parameter count that does not grow with the switch size.

We reformulate average-cost (ergodic) control of a Markov jump process as a finite-horizon stochastic optimal transport (SOT) problem that jointly optimizes over the controlled evolution and the marginal law from which it starts and returns to (i.e., a self-transport).
Exposing Noise-Deficiency Signatures of Deep Learning-Based False Data Injection Attacks Through AC State Estimation
Authors: Yichen Liu, Hongyu Wu, Bo Liu
First: 2026-10-22T18:40:14+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T18:40:14+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Deep learning (DL)-based false data injection (FDI) attacks can resemble normal measurements and evade both conventional bad data detection (BDD) and measurement-space data-driven detectors. This paper identifies a previously overlooked signature of such attacks in state-estimation (SE) residual space. The residuals of reconstruction-based DL attacks exhibit deficient stochastic variation, manifested as abnormally small residual energy and reduced directional dispersion. We establish the conditions under which this signature emerges and explain why the attacks carrying it can evade conventional BDD. We show that SE can reveal residual-space differences obscured by operating-state variation in measurement space. We then explain the origin of the signature through the noise-suppression mechanism of DL attack generation. A generator can learn the reproducible structure of historical measurements but cannot reproduce stochastic measurement components that are unpredictable from its input. This signature enables label-free detection using a simple two-sided test on the conventional BDD statistic, while the full residual vector can also be exploited when labeled attack samples are available. Experiments on the IEEE 14-bus and 118-bus AC systems show that the label-free test detects up to 99% of three representative DL attacks at a false-alarm rate comparable to that of BDD, which detects at most 5%. Ablation studies further examine the effects of residual representation, decision rule, and measurement-noise magnitude and distribution.
Summary / 总结
Deep learning (DL)-based false data injection (FDI) attacks can resemble normal measurements and evade both conventional bad data detection (BDD) and measurement-space data-driven detectors.
Asset-Normalized Distribution Reliability Budget Optimization Using Historical Outage Benefits and Work-Type Learning
Authors: Hritik Gopal Shah, Catherine Tajmajer
First: 2026-10-22T18:07:05+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T18:07:05+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Reliability investments compete for constrained capital but are often evaluated in separate programs. This paper presents a data-driven framework that translates historical work orders, geographic asset records, outage events, and costs into comparable customer-minute and customer-interruption benefits at the circuit-zone level. A normalization process maps historical device names and non-isolating assets to current automaticisolating-device zones and links investments to pre- and postconstruction outage histories. Utility data covering more than $250 million in investments completed from 2010 to 2020 are used to estimate benefit factors for seven work types. Those factors feed a mixed-integer portfolio model that selects at most one applicable action per zone under a capital budget. Observed reductions range from 32% to 94% for customer-minutes interrupted and from 43% to 93% for customer interruptions. The framework provides a traceable path from heterogeneous utility records to zone-level reliability investment decisions

Model Predictive Control of Wind Turbines: A Model-Oriented Review of Prediction Models, Control Objectives, and Nonlinear Aerodynamic Formulations
Authors: Roya Khalili-Amirabadi, Mohsen Jalaeian-Farimani, Mahdi Ghane
First: 2026-10-22T13:58:04+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T13:58:04+00:00

Journal of Intelligent Monitoring and Control Systems, 1(1): 35-41. https://doi.org/10.65098/jimcs.01.2026.35.41
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Model predictive control (MPC) has been applied to wind-turbine regulation with markedly different internal prediction models, ranging from linearized low-order turbine models to nonlinear, aeroelastic, floating, and data-driven predictors. This review organizes the literature primarily by the model propagated by the controller and treats model order, predictor representation, MPC formulation, control objective, and validation plant as separate descriptors. Primary studies show that low-order linear or piecewise-affine predictors are attractive for constrained power and speed regulation, whereas flexible drivetrain, structural, and floating models are needed when drivetrain twist, tower motion, blade loads, or platform dynamics are explicit control quantities. Nonlinear and economic MPC studies further demonstrate coordinated pitch/torque control across broad operating regions, while preview, repetitive, and data-driven formulations address wind prediction, periodic loads, and model mismatch. Particular attention is given to aerodynamic pitch dependence: variable-pitch turbine dynamics can be non-affine with respect to applied pitch, and the term NMPC alone does not prove that the original nonlinear pitch map is retained inside the optimizer. The review therefore adopts a conservative, equation-based classification for direct nonlinear applied-pitch propagation. Here, direct nonlinear applied-pitch propagation means that the applied pitch at each prediction step is evaluated inside the original nonlinear aerodynamic map, rather than replacing that dependence by a linearized, scheduled, piecewise-affine, or learned surrogate. The literature supports a remaining integration challenge at the intersection of flexible drivetrain prediction, nonlinear aerodynamic pitch treatment, explicit constraints and load objectives, and validated adaptive or learned model correction.

Large Language Models Meet Bayesian Optimization: Towards Self-evolving Wireless Digital Twins
Authors: Yingzhuo Sun, Haoyun Li, Ming Xiao, Francisco J. Ferrao, Berna Ozbek
First: 2026-10-22T12:40:49+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T12:40:49+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Wireless digital twins (DTs) are increasingly integrated with artificial intelligence to support online modeling, prediction, and network optimization. However, most existing approaches perform adaptation within predefined learning or optimization structures, which may become unsuitable when wireless tasks and operating conditions change. In this article, we investigate the integration of large language models (LLMs) and Bayesian optimization (BO) to extend wireless DTs from online adaptation towards algorithm-level self-evolution. We introduce an LLM-assisted BO framework in which BO provides a stable and sample-efficient optimization backbone, while the LLM determines whether selected BO components should be retained or adapted based on the current task and accumulated optimization experience. In simulations, we apply the framework to Gaussian process (GP) kernel discovery for a multi-user wireless beam-selection problem. Simulation results show that the LLM-discovered kernel improves the final BO performance by 19.7% on the training tasks compared with the strongest standard kernel baseline. The results demonstrate the potential of combining LLM flexibility with structured BO for self-evolving wireless DTs.

PriMa-Causa: Causal Prescriptive Maintenance
Authors: Felix Saretzky
First: 2026-10-22T12:06:59+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T12:06:59+00:00
Comments: PhD thesis, University of Luxembourg, 2026. 203 pages. Also available at https://hdl.handle.net/10993/69383
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Automated manufacturing requires data-driven decisions under uncertainty and sparse observational records, and unplanned downtime is a dominant cost of getting them wrong. Prescriptive maintenance recommends a maintenance action against an impending failure rather than merely predicting it. Established reference architectures couple predictive models with rule- or optimisation-based recommendation logic. Selecting a corrective action, however, requires an interventional quantity: how the line behaviour would change if the action were set. Manufacturing data is observational, so a model fitted to predict the outcome estimates an associational quantity and may misdirect the choice of action without the prediction error revealing it. Causal estimators target the interventional quantity but require an expert-led causal study per case, for which the bounded decision window of operation leaves no room. This dissertation analyses how causal reasoning can be embedded into prescriptive maintenance so that actions are ranked by their estimated interventional effect. The result is PriMa-Causa, built from three components: a causal reasoning layer that integrates the estimate into the PriMa reference model, a parametric simulator that supplies the interventional ground truth absent from manufacturing data, and a graph-conditioned tabular causal foundation model that, pre-trained once on this synthetic production-line prior, answers interventional queries in a single forward pass. PriMa-Causa attains a lower prescription error than associational baselines, with the largest margin under weak overlap and sparse context, and a partially disclosed causal graph reduces this error further. The causal inductive bias of the synthetic prior and the graph conditioning can thus replace per-case estimator fitting, a step toward automating causal inference for autonomous and self-healing maintenance.
Summary / 总结
Automated manufacturing requires data-driven decisions under uncertainty and sparse observational records, and unplanned downtime is a dominant cost of getting them wrong.
