Lyapunov Exponents in Power Systems: A Survey of Theory and Applications in Stability, Control, Forecasting, and Protection
Authors: Mohammad Almomani, Venkataramana Ajjarapu
First: 2026-10-16T17:33:01+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-16T17:33:01+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Lyapunov exponents (LEs) are fundamental metrics from nonlinear dynamical systems theory that quantify the average exponential rate of divergence or convergence of nearby trajectories in phase space. Over the past three decades, LEs have emerged as powerful tools across a wide range of power system problems, including transient and voltage stability assessment, chaos detection and mitigation, short-term load and renewable energy forecasting, and fault diagnosis. This paper presents a comprehensive review of these applications, covering more than 165 publications spanning from the early demonstrations of chaos in simplified machine models (1990s) to the most recent data-driven and machine-learning-augmented approaches applied to inverter-based resource (IBR)-dominated grids (2024--2026). The review is organized into four principal application domains: (i) stability analysis and real-time monitoring, (ii) chaos characterization and control, (iii) forecasting and predictability analysis, and (iv) protection, fault diagnosis, and condition monitoring. For each domain, we trace the evolution of methods, compare model-based and data-driven LE estimation strategies, identify key benchmarks and validation gaps, and outline the most pressing open research problems.

Regularization in Data-driven Predictive Control: A Convex Relaxation Perspective
Authors: Xu Shang, Yang Zheng
First: 2025-09-10T21:54:13+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-16T08:28:08+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
This paper explores the role of regularization in data-driven predictive control (DDPC) through the lens of convex relaxation. Using a bi-level optimization framework, we model system identification as an inner problem and predictive control as an outer problem. Within this framework, we show that several regularized DDPC formulations, including l1-norm penalties, projection-based regularizers, and a newly introduced causality-based regularizer, can be viewed as convex relaxations of their respective bi-level problems. This perspective clarifies the conceptual links between direct and indirect data-driven control and highlights how regularization implicitly enforces system identification. We further propose an optimality-based variant, A-DDPC, which approximately solves the inner problem with all identification constraints via an iterative algorithm. Numerical experiments demonstrate that A-DDPC outperforms existing regularized DDPC by reducing both bias and variance errors. These results indicate that further benefits may be obtained by applying system identification techniques to pre-process the trajectory library in nonlinear settings. Overall, our analysis contributes to a unified convex relaxation view of regularization in DDPC and sheds light on its strong empirical performance beyond linear time-invariant systems.

Data Center Load Accommodation under QoS and Checkpoint-Induced Frequency Security Constraints
Authors: Ruolan Cui, Ziang Liu, Chenxi Hu, Francis Yunhe Hou
First: 2026-10-16T04:20:49+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-16T04:20:49+00:00
Comments: 10 pages
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
The rapid growth of artificial intelligence workloads is accelerating the expansion and grid interconnection of data centers (DCs). Existing data center load accommodation (DCLA) assessments primarily focus on grid and resource adequacy and often imply that greater workload flexibility always increases accommodable capacity. However, workload flexibility has a dual effect. Workload deferral and temporal shifting can alleviate network congestion, whereas synchronized checkpointing of training workloads can cause large and rapid load reductions, threatening frequency stability. Therefore, DCLA cannot be determined solely by grid adequacy. This paper develops an optimal DCLA method that jointly accounts for workload quality of service (QoS), checkpoint-induced frequency security (FS), and grid adequacy. First, a QoS-aware model characterizes workload-dependent flexibility while ensuring service feasibility. Second, checkpoint-induced FS constraints are developed for periodic and event-triggered power variations, with key frequency-security limits reformulated in algebraic form. Third, a two-stage lexicographic model is developed to maximize DCLA and minimizes the required energy storage (ES) deployment, and then optimizes system operation under the given DC and ES siting and sizing decisions. Case studies show that the proposed method avoids a 40.3% overestimation of DCLA compared with the grid-adequacy benchmark. QoS is the primary limiting factor, while checkpoint-induced frequency-security constraints become more restrictive as training workloads increase. These findings highlight that reliable DCLA should account for both its grid-support value and the associated QoS and FS risks.

The rapid growth of artificial intelligence workloads is accelerating the expansion and grid interconnection of data centers (DCs).
ffconnect: An Interactive Interface for Control Integration in Mid-Fidelity Wind Farm Simulation
Authors: Zekai Chen, Casey Heiskell, Ryozo Nagamune
First: 2026-07-17T17:38:41+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T23:25:36+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Wind farm control (WFC) plays a crucial role in mitigating the wake effect, the negative aerodynamic interactions among wind turbines. Recent advances in data-driven control and artificial intelligence offer new opportunities to design more intelligent WFC systems, motivating the need for a tool that supports interactive design and validation in simulation. To address this, we present ffconnect, a general, open-source Python-based interface for FAST.Farm, a mid-fidelity wind farm simulator. Compared to prior work, ffconnect introduces a restructured Application Programming Interface (API) with enriched state access and supports integrating FAST.Farm with modern scientific computing and machine learning ecosystems by building entirely on Python. In experiments, ffconnect shows negligible runtime overhead compared to the original FAST.Farm across a range of simulation lengths and farm sizes, and demonstrates its effectiveness through a yaw-tracking case study. Finally, we provide the source code of ffconnect to keep it accessible for general users.

Koopman operator learning for predictive control via Khatri-Rao kernel regression
Authors: Mircea Lazar
First: 2026-06-01T22:38:23+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T20:59:24+00:00
Comments: Revised paper: updated the simulation section with comparison against linear Koopman models and clarified several implementation aspects
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
This paper develops a data-driven realization of the generalized Koopman operator (GeKo) [1], in which states and inputs are lifted independently and the dynamics are expressed as a tensor bilinear system. The first contribution is a time-sequenced multi-step Khatri-Rao kernel regression formulation that exposes the operator to evolved snapshots along trajectories rather than only single one-step pairs, which reduces compounded prediction error. Secondly, we develop a kernel- and input-agnostic structured SVD reduction that compresses the lifted state and input spaces while preserving the Khatri-Rao realization. We instantiate the framework with random Fourier features and describe a complete predictive-control pipeline, including a multi-step roll-out diagnostic that guides the choice of MPC horizon. The framework is validated on the chaotic Lorenz system, where the learned reduced-order GeKo model reduces the long-horizon prediction error by up to a factor of five relative to one-step and lifted linear baselines fitted on the same data, and stabilizes an unstable equilibrium.

Stability Analysis in IBR-Dominated Grids: Model-Based and Data-Driven Methodologies
Authors: Federico Cecati, Yang Wu, Florian Dörfler, Marco Liserre, Xiongfei Wang, Elisabetta Tedeschi
First: 2026-10-15T17:06:49+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T17:06:49+00:00
Comments: Submitted to IEEE Transactions on Power Electronics
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Stability issues in converter-dominated grids have a multifold nature, where static, small-signal and large-signal phenomena can influence each other. Capturing the interplay among these different stability aspects requires a rethinking of conventional modeling and analysis tools for power systems. As a further complication, Intellectual Property protection among manufacturers and system operators makes detailed converters and grid models unattainable. This motivates the use of data-driven approaches for stability analysis and control, nevertheless achieving an insightful representation of static, small-signal and large-signal phenomena from data still leaves many open questions. This paper discusses model-based and data-driven methodologies for stability analysis of converter-dominated grids. The instability mechanisms of static, small-signal and large-signal phenomena are conceptually explained, and the capability of different modeling and analysis tools to investigate them is examined. The effectiveness of data-driven methodologies to analyze instability phenomena in their multifold nature is debated, and the challenges on data completeness, model generalization under untrained conditions, physical interpretability, and uncertainty awareness are discussed.

Abstract
This paper proposes a novel approach to design analog electronic circuits that implement Model Predictive Control (MPC) policies for dynamical systems described by affine models. Effective approaches to define a reduced-complexity explicit MPC are combined and applied to realize an analog circuit comprising a limited set of low-latency, commercially available components. The practical feasibility and effectiveness of the proposed approach are demonstrated through its application in the design of a novel controller for DC-DC Buck converters. We formally analyze the stability of the resulting system and conduct extensive numerical simulations to demonstrate the control system's performance in rejecting line and load disturbances.

Defense Filters for Robust Learning of Partially Observed Dynamical Systems
Authors: Jihun Kim, Javad Lavaei
First: 2026-10-15T14:11:55+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T14:11:55+00:00
Comments: 8 pages
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Robust estimation of unknown system dynamics from partial observations under attack is a core challenge in learning for control, increasingly critical in multi-agent networks subject to adversarial data poisoning. Existing methods either require highly restrictive assumptions on the adversary or yield weak error bounds. In this paper, we consider identifying partially observed linear dynamical systems subject to adversarial attacks with an attack probability $p < 0.5$ at each time. We propose a defense-filtering framework in which a system designer leverages knowledge of the observation matrix to construct a defense filter satisfying full column rank and subspace separability conditions, which enables the evaluation of a single-attack scenario against observed data. Using this framework, we analyze the constrained $\ell_2$-norm estimator and establish finite-sample error bounds for recovering the Markov parameter matrix $G^*$. This is the first approach to provide provable system identification guarantees via a defense filter for partially observed systems subject to harsh attack environments where the attack probability approaches $0.5$.

Graph Neural Ordinary Differential Equations for Power System Identification with Transfer Learning Applications
Authors: Hannes M. H. Wolf, Christian A. Hans
First: 2026-04-27T09:17:51+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T08:44:10+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
With the shift towards decentralized energy generation, the increasing complexity of power systems renders physics-based modeling challenging. At the same time the growing amount of available measurement data opens the door for obtaining data-driven models. A modern method to do so are neural ordinary differential equations (NODEs), offering a framework for continuous-time system identification. Recent extensions, so called graph NODEs impose a structural inductive bias that has the potential to improve generalization of the learned representations. In this work, we employ and extend them to develop message-passing graph NODEs (MPG-NODEs) for identification of coupled systems with heterogeneous node dynamics and heterogeneous edge couplings. This includes state-of-the-art machine learning architectures to infer latent representations of unmeasured states, local node and edge embeddings to account for heterogeneity as well as an autoregressive scheme to allow for piecewise constant control inputs. We apply MPG-NODEs to identify voltage and frequency dynamics of power systems and compare them to a monolithic NODE under identical measurement assumptions. Our case study indicates that while achieving similar performance, our proposed MPG-NODEs are more flexible with transfer learning options that allow to add or remove edges and nodes with little retraining.

Learning neural controllers for nonlinear systems from data

Abstract
This article addresses the problem of designing neural feedback controllers for unknown nonlinear systems. We propose an indirect data-driven framework that uses offline data to identify the system dynamics, upon which a neural feedback controller and a neural Lyapunov function are jointly synthesized. Input constraints are enforced by integrating a hard-saturation structure into the controller architecture. Robust synthesis conditions are derived to account for data perturbations during identification. Formal stability is certified by combining SMT verification with local Lyapunov analysis near the equilibrium. Numerical examples validate the effectiveness of the proposed framework.
