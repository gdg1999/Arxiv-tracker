A research world model of power engineering learns its dynamics and reveals what-ifs
Authors: Fanping Sui, Ying Zhang, Liwei Lin
First: 2026-11-03T09:29:05+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-03T09:29:05+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Decisions about the direction of research in power engineering determine which technologies mature and, through them, shape infrastructure that lasts decades, yet their consequences cannot be observed before they are taken, and existing forecasts of research fields extrapolate a single trajectory. Here we introduce research world models, which treat a research field as a dynamical system that can be acted upon, and establish one for power engineering from 1.1 million papers. A distilled language model reads each paper into role-typed concepts, two sophisticated, high-fidelity graph-temporal neural network cores learn how the resulting concept network evolves, and operators learned from natural experiments in the publication record estimate the effects of interventions. In temporally held-out tests, the model forecast years ahead which concepts would grow and which method-target pairs would be joined for the first time, outperforming tuned machine-learning baselines in most settings, and its estimated intervention effects tracked those realized in natural experiments it had not seen. Its training scheme and three of its operators replicated in two fields withheld from development. Questions posed in natural language are compiled by a domain-specialized 27-billion-parameter language model into executable actions and answered as paired simulations with and without the intervention. The answers were rated 4.1 of 5 for faithfulness by an independent language-model judge. By revealing the latent dynamics of a research field and its response to interventions, research world models offer a new way to simulate and test research decisions before they are made.

Decisions about the direction of research in power engineering determine which technologies mature and, through them, shape infrastructure that lasts decades, yet their consequences cannot be observed before they are taken, and existing forecasts of research fields extrapolate a single trajectory.
A Review of Max-Pressure Traffic Signal Control Algorithms: State of the Art and Research Opportunities
Authors: Abdullah Al Asif, Chaopeng Tan, Vikash V. Gayah, Hao Liu
First: 2026-11-02T22:20:23+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-02T22:20:23+00:00
Comments: 49 pages, 7 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Max-Pressure (MP) has emerged as a widely studied decentralized traffic signal control framework, thanks to its desirable theoretical properties and strong control performance. Early research on MP focused on improving control performance by exploring different variables of the traffic state for the definition of pressure. Subsequent studies expanded MP to incorporate the complexity of real-world transportation systems and emerging technologies. This paper provides a comprehensive review of these developments. The review first introduces the theoretical foundations of MP and examines early developments in movement weight definition. It then synthesizes major extensions for multimodal and multiscale network control, followed by recent developments in connected and automated vehicle (CAV) environments and the integration of MP with artificial intelligence (AI) and data-driven methods. Beyond summarizing existing approaches, this review examines their underlying assumptions, data requirements, implementation considerations, and practical limitations. Based on this comprehensive review, key research gaps and future directions are identified, including remaining limitations in the theoretical foundations of MP and the underexplored integration of autonomous vehicle control with the MP framework. Overall, this review provides a structured perspective on the evolution and current state of MP control and highlights opportunities for developing more proactive, robust, and practically deployable MP-based traffic control methods.

Contingency-Aware Planning via Certified Neural Hamilton-Jacobi Reachability
Authors: Kasidit Muenprasitivej, Derya Aksaray
First: 2026-03-17T18:04:20+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-02T21:17:33+00:00
Comments: 9 pages, 4 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Hamilton-Jacobi (HJ) reachability provides formal safety guarantees for dynamical systems, but repeatedly solving the HJ partial differential equation in changing environments limits its use in real-time planning. This paper introduces learning-based reachability and an online safety certification that together enable real-time contingency-aware planning in unknown environments with probabilistic safety guarantees. We use Fourier Neural Operator (FNO) to approximate the HJ solution operator under varying obstacle configurations. To certify the learned reach-avoid set, we recast robust scenario optimization as multiple hypothesis testings over its sublevel sets, which yields a simultaneous guarantee across candidate levels and recovers the largest certifiable volume at the prescribed safety level. Within this certified set, the induced recovery policy is guaranteed to return the system to a safe region in finite time under any admissible disturbance. Numerical results show that the learning and certification pipeline updates reach-avoid sets at real-time rates and is demonstrated on youBot simulation.

A Quantitative Approach to Reach-Avoid Reachability Analysis for Discrete-Time Nonlinear Control Systems
Authors: Ihab Tabbara, Eliya Badr, Hussein Sibai
First: 2025-11-19T08:46:41+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-02T19:59:42+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
A reach-avoid specification requires a dynamical system to reach a target set of states while avoiding a failure set. Reachability analysis partitions the state space into parts starting from which the specification can be satisfied by following some control signal and those from which it cannot despite the best control effort. In this paper, we present a quantitative approach for reachability analysis to analyze the satisfaction of avoid and reach-avoid specifications for discrete-time nonlinear control systems. In such an approach, a value function assigns a value to each state that determines how robustly the system can satisfy the specification starting from it and following the optimal control signal. However, computing such a value function is difficult. Instead, our approach constructs discrete abstractions on which it performs value iteration to compute sound bounds on the desired value function. The resulting lower bound enables a sound under-approximation of the reach avoid set (RAS) and, in the avoid case, a sound over-approximation of the backward reachable set (BRS). When the control set is finite, the upper bound additionally enables a sound over-approximation of the RAS and, in the avoid case, a sound under-approximation of the BRS. The lower bound also yields quantitative guarantees on safety margins and safe target-reaching times. Our approach further refines the abstraction by splitting the grid cells that could not be classified as within or outside the BRS (or RAS) to improve the usefulness of the bounds. We evaluate our approach on five example systems with up to five-dimensional state spaces.

A reach-avoid specification requires a dynamical system to reach a target set of states while avoiding a failure set.
A Tutorial on Shape Servoing of Deformable Objects-Part II: Advanced Concepts
Authors: Ignacio Cuiral-Zueco, Alessio Caporali, Miguel Aranda, Gianluca Palli, Gonzalo López-Nicolás, Youcef Mezouar
First: 2026-11-02T19:03:00+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-02T19:03:00+00:00
Comments: Part II of a two-part tutorial. 10 pages, 9 figures. Code: https://github.com/nachocz/tutorial_on_shape_servoing_of_deformable_objects. Interactive demos: https://nachocz.github.io/tutorial_on_shape_servoing_of_deformable_objects/
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3 · Project1
Abstract
In robotics, shape control, or shape servoing, refers to the automatic manipulation of a deformable object toward a desired shape. This article is the second of a two-part tutorial on data-driven, Jacobian-based shape control, in which the deformation Jacobian, a local model of how robot motion changes the measured shape, is estimated from measurements. Part I built the shape-servoing loop and derived a local stability condition. This paper starts by using the singular value decomposition of the deformation Jacobian to identify shape changes that can be produced by small robot motions, those that require large robot motions, and those that are locally unavailable. It then analyses how the choice of shape features determines how the controller regulates the object's shape and, therefore, how much the object deforms on its way to the target. It then describes three ways in which the shape control loop can fail: loss of controllability, loss of observability, and occurrence of mechanical instabilities. It closes with shape-control extensions to time-varying targets, secondary tasks, and three-dimensional objects.

On the Comparison of Reinforcement Learning and Adaptive Control for Linear Systems under Packet Loss and Uncertainty
Authors: Moh Kamalul Wafi
First: 2026-06-30T17:36:20+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-02T17:12:22+00:00
Comments: Extended and revised version of the paper published in the Proceedings of the SIAM Conference on Control and Its Applications (CT23). doi.org/10.1137/1.9781611977745
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
This paper presents a comparative study between Adaptive Quantized Control (AQC) and Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (DDPG) reinforcement learning for uncertain linear systems with input quantization over communication channels subject to packet loss. The considered setting also includes dynamic switching from a nominal unstable system to a more unstable one during operation. The AQC is designed for unknown system dynamics using acknowledgment messages to compensate for packet losses, whereas the DDPG controller is trained using the nominal system model without acknowledgment messages. Numerical results show that the DDPG controller achieves faster transient responses and improved damping within its training environment. However, under model uncertainty, packet loss, and dynamic switching, the AQC consistently demonstrates superior robustness owing to its rigorous Lyapunov stability guarantees. These results highlight the trade-off between data-driven performance and model-based robustness, and provide insight into the applicability of reinforcement learning and adaptive control for networked uncertain systems.

Algebraic Characterization of Time-Varying Set Invariance
Authors: S. B. Alaoui, E. J. Wafo Wembe, A. Saoud
First: 2026-11-02T16:00:32+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-02T16:00:32+00:00
Comments: The paper was accepted for publication in the CDC 2026
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
The verification of temporal forward invariance for dynamical systems subject to time-varying constraints is a foundational problem in the analysis of safety-critical systems. Existing methodologies rely on restrictive geometric assumptions, such as the Linear Independence Constraint Qualification (LICQ), or suffer from exponential computational complexity when applied to time-varying polytopes. This paper establishes a rigorous, unified, and computationally tractable framework for temporal safety verification. First, utilizing tools from nonsmooth analysis, we prove that a Nagumo-type geometric inclusion evaluated strictly on the boundary of the augmented graph is both necessary and sufficient for temporal forward invariance. Second, we provide an algebraic characterization of this condition requiring only the weaker Abadie Constraint Qualification (ACQ), generalizing standard Control Barrier Function (CBF) boundary conditions to non-smooth, multi-constraint domains. Finally, for the practical class of Linear Time-Invariant (LTI) systems with time-varying polytopic constraints, we deploy Farkas' Lemma to eliminate exponential vertex enumeration. By introducing a Lipschitz-bounded time-discretization scheme, we reduce the infinite-dimensional continuous-time verification problem to a finite, highly tractable set of decoupled Linear Programs (LPs).

Residual-Corrected Equivalent-Circuit Model with Universal Differential Equations for Robust Battery Voltage Prediction under Operating-Condition Shift
Authors: Alexandre Barbosa de Lima, Roberta Vieira Raggi

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Accurate terminal-voltage prediction underpins model-based battery management, yet low-order equivalent-circuit models (\ecm{}) lack expressiveness under transient conditions, whereas purely data-driven predictors sacrifice interpretability and may degrade under operating-condition shift. This paper introduces a residual-corrected hybrid formulation in which a first-order Thevenin \ecm{} (\ecmrc{}) provides the dominant voltage structure, and a compact neural network embedded as a universal differential equation (\ude{}) corrects only the latent polarization mismatch. The \ecmrc{} parameters identified by nonlinear least squares warm-start the hybrid model so that the learned component operates in a low-residual regime. Experiments on a public Panasonic 18650PF dataset compare the proposed \ecmude{} with standalone \ecmrc{} and Long Short-Term Memory (\lstm{}) baselines across four axes: matched-condition prediction on UDDS at \SI{25}{\celsius}, inference-time perturbation of the supplied state-of-charge (\SOC{}, denoted $z$) input, zero-shot temperature transfer (\SI{25}{\celsius} to \SI{-20}{\celsius}), and zero-shot drive-cycle transfer to US06, LA92, and HWFET. The proposed \ecmude{} achieves the lowest voltage error in every setting, reducing mean absolute error (\mae{}) by 48\% relative to the \lstm{} under matched conditions and showing an order-of-magnitude lower inter-seed variability (coefficient of variation: 0.44\% vs.\ 6.20\%). Substantial gains persist under challenging distribution shifts, indicating that the physical model anchors prediction where a purely learned model is most vulnerable. These results position residual-corrected \ecmude{} as a lightweight and interpretable enhancement of low-order circuit models for voltage prediction in battery management systems (\bms{}).

Shadow Pricing of Static Voltage Stability Services within Unit Commitment for Inverter-Dominated Power Systems
Authors: Peng Wang, Luis Badesa
First: 2026-07-06T15:27:18+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-02T11:27:12+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Modern power systems are increasingly dominated by Inverter-Based Resources (IBR), most of which work in Grid-following (GFL) mode. This implies that they do not directly control their terminal voltage, so the static voltage stability at these buses may be compromised, especially under constant-power-factor operation that lacks voltage-adaptive reactive support. In addition, weather-driven IBR are often installed in electrically remote areas with low Short-Circuit Ratio (SCR), further exacerbating voltage issues. To address this challenge, grid-forming control can be utilized to enhance low-SCR buses, while GFL-IBR could be explicitly required to provide voltage support through grid codes. As an alternative, a market mechanism could be devised that incentivizes relevant generators to proactively adjust their operating points as a service to maintain voltage stability, while the theoretical framework for such a market has not been developed. To fill this gap, this work adopts a second-order cone-based static voltage stability constraint for GFL-IBR buses within a unit commitment problem, and proposes a mechanism to assign shadow prices to this ancillary service. To determine appropriate price values under non-convex conditions, different pricing schemes are assessed. Using a modified IEEE 30-bus system, we demonstrate that both the dispatchable and restricted pricing methods can yield revenue-adequate service prices, though the former may deliver less efficient price signals and the latter may require well-defined uplift payments. This implies that, given differentiated pricing mechanisms and price signals, operators need to select a suitable pricing method in accordance with actual system conditions and market rules.

A Universal Policy Wrapper With Guarantees
Authors: Georgiy Malaniya, Anton Bolychev, Pavel Osinenko
First: 2026-11-02T10:22:46+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-02T10:22:46+00:00
Comments: Accepted for IEEE Access (2026)
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Many control tasks require driving the system into a prescribed goal set and subsequently keeping it there. The considered setting assumes that a working controller is available that provably reaches and maintains the goal, albeit with suboptimal performance. This controller is referred to as the fallback policy. Conversely, although state-of-the-art reinforcement-learning (RL) algorithms can discover policies with impressive empirical performance, the neural networks at their core act as opaque black boxes learned through data-driven exploration and, as such, provide no theoretical mechanism to guarantee that the system will ever reach---and stay within---the prescribed goal set. This article presents CALF-Wrapper, a method that combines the fallback policy with a pretrained RL base policy and uses the critic estimate associated with the base policy to select between them during deployment. The theoretical analysis establishes that CALF-Wrapper inherits the goal-reaching property of the fallback policy under the stated assumptions and provides probability bounds for the settling time and the maximum distance from the goal set over a prescribed time interval. The experimental study evaluates CALF-Wrapper in four simulated control environments of different complexity. Comparisons include the base policy, fallback policy, and constrained reinforcement-learning methods formulated for the same objective. Sensitivity and ablation studies provide direct hyperparameter recommendations. The experiments show how CALF-Wrapper can provide effective task performance while attaining the maximum possible observed goal-reaching rate. CALF-Wrapper requires no additional dynamics model, online learning, or constrained optimization during execution.
Summary / 总结
Many control tasks require driving the system into a prescribed goal set and subsequently keeping it there.
