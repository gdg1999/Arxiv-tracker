Stability Analysis in IBR-Dominated Grids: Model-Based and Data-Driven Methodologies
Authors: Federico Cecati, Yang Wu, Florian Dörfler, Marco Liserre, Xiongfei Wang, Elisabetta Tedeschi
First: 2026-10-15T17:06:49+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T17:06:49+00:00
Comments: Submitted to IEEE Transactions on Power Electronics
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Stability issues in converter-dominated grids have a multifold nature, where static, small-signal and large-signal phenomena can influence each other. Capturing the interplay among these different stability aspects requires a rethinking of conventional modeling and analysis tools for power systems. As a further complication, Intellectual Property protection among manufacturers and system operators makes detailed converters and grid models unattainable. This motivates the use of data-driven approaches for stability analysis and control, nevertheless achieving an insightful representation of static, small-signal and large-signal phenomena from data still leaves many open questions. This paper discusses model-based and data-driven methodologies for stability analysis of converter-dominated grids. The instability mechanisms of static, small-signal and large-signal phenomena are conceptually explained, and the capability of different modeling and analysis tools to investigate them is examined. The effectiveness of data-driven methodologies to analyze instability phenomena in their multifold nature is debated, and the challenges on data completeness, model generalization under untrained conditions, physical interpretability, and uncertainty awareness are discussed.

A Fully Analog Implementation of Model Predictive Control with Application to Buck Converters
Authors: Simone Pirrera, Lorenzo Calogero, Francesco Gabriele, Diego Regruto, Alessandro Rizzo, Gianluca Setti
First: 2025-09-05T19:35:30+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T17:01:09+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
This paper proposes a novel approach to design analog electronic circuits that implement Model Predictive Control (MPC) policies for dynamical systems described by affine models. Effective approaches to define a reduced-complexity explicit MPC are combined and applied to realize an analog circuit comprising a limited set of low-latency, commercially available components. The practical feasibility and effectiveness of the proposed approach are demonstrated through its application in the design of a novel controller for DC-DC Buck converters. We formally analyze the stability of the resulting system and conduct extensive numerical simulations to demonstrate the control system's performance in rejecting line and load disturbances.

Defense Filters for Robust Learning of Partially Observed Dynamical Systems
Authors: Jihun Kim, Javad Lavaei
First: 2026-10-15T14:11:55+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T14:11:55+00:00
Comments: 8 pages
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Robust estimation of unknown system dynamics from partial observations under attack is a core challenge in learning for control, increasingly critical in multi-agent networks subject to adversarial data poisoning. Existing methods either require highly restrictive assumptions on the adversary or yield weak error bounds. In this paper, we consider identifying partially observed linear dynamical systems subject to adversarial attacks with an attack probability $p < 0.5$ at each time. We propose a defense-filtering framework in which a system designer leverages knowledge of the observation matrix to construct a defense filter satisfying full column rank and subspace separability conditions, which enables the evaluation of a single-attack scenario against observed data. Using this framework, we analyze the constrained $\ell_2$-norm estimator and establish finite-sample error bounds for recovering the Markov parameter matrix $G^*$. This is the first approach to provide provable system identification guarantees via a defense filter for partially observed systems subject to harsh attack environments where the attack probability approaches $0.5$.

Robust estimation of unknown system dynamics from partial observations under attack is a core challenge in learning for control, increasingly critical in multi-agent networks subject to adversarial data poisoning.
Graph Neural Ordinary Differential Equations for Power System Identification with Transfer Learning Applications
Authors: Hannes M. H. Wolf, Christian A. Hans
First: 2026-04-27T09:17:51+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T08:44:10+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
With the shift towards decentralized energy generation, the increasing complexity of power systems renders physics-based modeling challenging. At the same time the growing amount of available measurement data opens the door for obtaining data-driven models. A modern method to do so are neural ordinary differential equations (NODEs), offering a framework for continuous-time system identification. Recent extensions, so called graph NODEs impose a structural inductive bias that has the potential to improve generalization of the learned representations. In this work, we employ and extend them to develop message-passing graph NODEs (MPG-NODEs) for identification of coupled systems with heterogeneous node dynamics and heterogeneous edge couplings. This includes state-of-the-art machine learning architectures to infer latent representations of unmeasured states, local node and edge embeddings to account for heterogeneity as well as an autoregressive scheme to allow for piecewise constant control inputs. We apply MPG-NODEs to identify voltage and frequency dynamics of power systems and compare them to a monolithic NODE under identical measurement assumptions. Our case study indicates that while achieving similar performance, our proposed MPG-NODEs are more flexible with transfer learning options that allow to add or remove edges and nodes with little retraining.

With the shift towards decentralized energy generation, the increasing complexity of power systems renders physics-based modeling challenging.
Learning neural controllers for nonlinear systems from data
Authors: Zhongjie Hu, Zhi-Wei Liu, Chen Wang
First: 2026-08-29T14:42:58+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T07:16:38+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
This article addresses the problem of designing neural feedback controllers for unknown nonlinear systems. We propose an indirect data-driven framework that uses offline data to identify the system dynamics, upon which a neural feedback controller and a neural Lyapunov function are jointly synthesized. Input constraints are enforced by integrating a hard-saturation structure into the controller architecture. Robust synthesis conditions are derived to account for data perturbations during identification. Formal stability is certified by combining SMT verification with local Lyapunov analysis near the equilibrium. Numerical examples validate the effectiveness of the proposed framework.

Robust Data-Driven Controller Synthesis from Sampled and Uncertain Frequency Response Estimates using Integral Quadratic Constraints
Authors: Luuk Marceau Spin, Maurice W. P. M. H. Heemels, Nathan van de Wouw, Roland Toth
First: 2026-10-14T14:21:18+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T14:21:18+00:00
Comments: 15 pages, 6 figures, none other publications
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
In this paper, we develop a novel MIMO controller synthesis method that relies solely on frequency-domain data of discrete-time systems. The approach is based on an iterative design procedure using \emph{integral quadratic constraints} (IQCs). It generalizes existing data-driven methods that approximate semi-infinite IQC conditions by enforcing them on a finite set of measured frequency points. Such approximations do not provide guarantees on the continuous frequency axis and may fail to capture inter-frequency behavior. We address this limitation by formulating a convex optimization problem that preserves robustness guarantees over the entire frequency range. In particular, the proposed method explicitly accounts for inter-frequency behavior of both plant and controller, leading to certified robust stability and performance. The effectiveness and computational efficiency of the approach are demonstrated using experimentally obtained frequency response data from industrial-grade semiconductor equipment. The synthesized controller is implemented on the physical setup, and its performance is validated in real-time experiments.

In this paper, we develop a novel MIMO controller synthesis method that relies solely on frequency-domain data of discrete-time systems.
Sequential Learning-Based Physics-Informed Neural Network Modeling for Parameter Estimation on a Power Converter
Authors: Samuela Rokocakau, Panagiotis Kakosimos, Giulia Tresca, Pericle Zanchetta
First: 2026-10-14T11:48:19+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T11:48:19+00:00
Comments: IEEE ECCE 2026
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
A staged physics-informed neural network (PINN) is proposed for identifying the inductance, capacitance and parasitic resistances of a DC buck converter from time-domain data. Gradients are released in the order of L to C to rL to rC, a sequence derived from sensitivity analysis. The five-stage schedule recovers L and C to within 5% and the series ESR to 10%, while a three-stage variant attains the same accuracy 11% faster. An 84 factorial sweep confirms that the trained PINN remains accurate for simultaneous 8% perturbations of all four components, achieving a waveform RMSE of 2.6 x 10-2 over 5 ms-an order-of-magnitude better than a simultaneous-training baseline.

On the Mechanism of Multi-Cluster System Separation Under Extreme Events
Authors: Nikolaos Sofos, Georgios Tzounas, Federico Milano
First: 2026-10-14T10:09:19+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T10:09:19+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Historical blackout events demonstrate that, under complex multi-event contingencies, interconnected power systems can split into multiple areas exhibiting distinct dynamic behavior while remaining physically connected. This work provides a rationale for this phenomenon. Typical N-1 contingency analysis leads to a response dominated by a single unstable mode, supporting the two-cluster conjecture used by transient stability assessment tools such as the SIME method. However, extreme events, often involving cascading N-k contingencies, can excite multiple unstable modes. We establish a proof of concept based on linear modal expansion to show how such concurrent excitation leads to multiple coherent areas. The resulting behavior is illustrated through simulations on the standard WSCC 9-bus, IEEE 14-bus, and IEEE 39-bus systems, demonstrating the limitations of conventional two-cluster representations under extreme events.

Iteratively Convex Frequency Data-Driven Synthesis of Structured MIMO Controllers
Authors: Luuk Marceau Spin, Thom Samuels, Dragan Kostic, Maurice W. P. M. H. Heemels, Nathan van de Wouw, Roland Toth
First: 2026-10-14T08:11:08+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T08:11:08+00:00
Comments: 16 pages, 11 figures, TCST paper
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Frequency-response-function-based controller design enables synthesis directly from measured data, avoiding the need for accurate parametric plant models. This paper presents a generalized IQC-based approach for convex synthesis of structured MIMO feedback and feedforward controllers from frequency response function data. The framework supports flexible controller parametrizations, multiple performance objectives, and both singular-value and element-wise performance shaping of MIMO controllers within a unified frequency-domain formulation. To facilitate practical deployment, the synthesis approach is embedded within a bootstrapped controller design methodology that progressively increases controller flexibility while refining the performance specifications. The resulting approach is validated experimentally on the motion stage of an industrial wirebonder, where clear performance improvement is demonstrated through MIMO $\mathcal{H}_\infty$ synthesis, element-wise feedback shaping, and dynamic feedforward design. The results demonstrate that high-performance multivariable controllers can be synthesized directly from measured FRF data in a practical and systematic manner.

Runtime Monitoring of Supermartingale Certificates
Authors: Mirco Giacobbe, Thomas A. Henzinger, Konstantin Kueffner, Emily Yu, Đorđe Žikelić
First: 2026-10-14T07:26:00+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T07:26:00+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Supermartingale certificates formally guarantee temporal properties of discrete and continuous-time stochastic dynamical systems. However, these guarantees rely on a mathematical model of the world, leaving a sim-to-real gap that can invalidate them for the deployed system. We formulate the runtime monitoring problem for supermartingale certificates, in which a monitor observes a single system trajectory and raises an alarm when there is sufficient statistical evidence that the supermartingale certificate has been violated during execution. We present an algorithm to automatically construct a monitor using betting-based sequential tests that bound the probability of ever raising a false alarm at a user-specified level. Within this betting framework, we formulate strategies that allow the monitor to exploit spatial regularity to improve detection power while preserving this statistical guarantee. We demonstrate the efficacy of our approach on supermartingale certificate benchmarks from the literature.
Summary / 总结
Supermartingale certificates formally guarantee temporal properties of discrete and continuous-time stochastic dynamical systems.
