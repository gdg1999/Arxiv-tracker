Robust Data-Driven Controller Synthesis from Sampled and Uncertain Frequency Response Estimates using Integral Quadratic Constraints
Authors: Luuk Marceau Spin, Maurice W. P. M. H. Heemels, Nathan van de Wouw, Roland Toth
First: 2026-10-14T14:21:18+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T14:21:18+00:00
Comments: 15 pages, 6 figures, none other publications
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
In this paper, we develop a novel MIMO controller synthesis method that relies solely on frequency-domain data of discrete-time systems. The approach is based on an iterative design procedure using \emph{integral quadratic constraints} (IQCs). It generalizes existing data-driven methods that approximate semi-infinite IQC conditions by enforcing them on a finite set of measured frequency points. Such approximations do not provide guarantees on the continuous frequency axis and may fail to capture inter-frequency behavior. We address this limitation by formulating a convex optimization problem that preserves robustness guarantees over the entire frequency range. In particular, the proposed method explicitly accounts for inter-frequency behavior of both plant and controller, leading to certified robust stability and performance. The effectiveness and computational efficiency of the approach are demonstrated using experimentally obtained frequency response data from industrial-grade semiconductor equipment. The synthesized controller is implemented on the physical setup, and its performance is validated in real-time experiments.

Sequential Learning-Based Physics-Informed Neural Network Modeling for Parameter Estimation on a Power Converter
Authors: Samuela Rokocakau, Panagiotis Kakosimos, Giulia Tresca, Pericle Zanchetta
First: 2026-10-14T11:48:19+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T11:48:19+00:00
Comments: IEEE ECCE 2026
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
A staged physics-informed neural network (PINN) is proposed for identifying the inductance, capacitance and parasitic resistances of a DC buck converter from time-domain data. Gradients are released in the order of L to C to rL to rC, a sequence derived from sensitivity analysis. The five-stage schedule recovers L and C to within 5% and the series ESR to 10%, while a three-stage variant attains the same accuracy 11% faster. An 84 factorial sweep confirms that the trained PINN remains accurate for simultaneous 8% perturbations of all four components, achieving a waveform RMSE of 2.6 x 10-2 over 5 ms-an order-of-magnitude better than a simultaneous-training baseline.

On the Mechanism of Multi-Cluster System Separation Under Extreme Events
Authors: Nikolaos Sofos, Georgios Tzounas, Federico Milano
First: 2026-10-14T10:09:19+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T10:09:19+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Historical blackout events demonstrate that, under complex multi-event contingencies, interconnected power systems can split into multiple areas exhibiting distinct dynamic behavior while remaining physically connected. This work provides a rationale for this phenomenon. Typical N-1 contingency analysis leads to a response dominated by a single unstable mode, supporting the two-cluster conjecture used by transient stability assessment tools such as the SIME method. However, extreme events, often involving cascading N-k contingencies, can excite multiple unstable modes. We establish a proof of concept based on linear modal expansion to show how such concurrent excitation leads to multiple coherent areas. The resulting behavior is illustrated through simulations on the standard WSCC 9-bus, IEEE 14-bus, and IEEE 39-bus systems, demonstrating the limitations of conventional two-cluster representations under extreme events.

Iteratively Convex Frequency Data-Driven Synthesis of Structured MIMO Controllers
Authors: Luuk Marceau Spin, Thom Samuels, Dragan Kostic, Maurice W. P. M. H. Heemels, Nathan van de Wouw, Roland Toth
First: 2026-10-14T08:11:08+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T08:11:08+00:00
Comments: 16 pages, 11 figures, TCST paper
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Frequency-response-function-based controller design enables synthesis directly from measured data, avoiding the need for accurate parametric plant models. This paper presents a generalized IQC-based approach for convex synthesis of structured MIMO feedback and feedforward controllers from frequency response function data. The framework supports flexible controller parametrizations, multiple performance objectives, and both singular-value and element-wise performance shaping of MIMO controllers within a unified frequency-domain formulation. To facilitate practical deployment, the synthesis approach is embedded within a bootstrapped controller design methodology that progressively increases controller flexibility while refining the performance specifications. The resulting approach is validated experimentally on the motion stage of an industrial wirebonder, where clear performance improvement is demonstrated through MIMO $\mathcal{H}_\infty$ synthesis, element-wise feedback shaping, and dynamic feedforward design. The results demonstrate that high-performance multivariable controllers can be synthesized directly from measured FRF data in a practical and systematic manner.

Runtime Monitoring of Supermartingale Certificates
Authors: Mirco Giacobbe, Thomas A. Henzinger, Konstantin Kueffner, Emily Yu, Đorđe Žikelić
First: 2026-10-14T07:26:00+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T07:26:00+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Supermartingale certificates formally guarantee temporal properties of discrete and continuous-time stochastic dynamical systems. However, these guarantees rely on a mathematical model of the world, leaving a sim-to-real gap that can invalidate them for the deployed system. We formulate the runtime monitoring problem for supermartingale certificates, in which a monitor observes a single system trajectory and raises an alarm when there is sufficient statistical evidence that the supermartingale certificate has been violated during execution. We present an algorithm to automatically construct a monitor using betting-based sequential tests that bound the probability of ever raising a false alarm at a user-specified level. Within this betting framework, we formulate strategies that allow the monitor to exploit spatial regularity to improve detection power while preserving this statistical guarantee. We demonstrate the efficacy of our approach on supermartingale certificate benchmarks from the literature.

Supermartingale certificates formally guarantee temporal properties of discrete and continuous-time stochastic dynamical systems.
Identifiability-Guided Composite Load Modeling of AI Data Centers
Authors: Ding Lin, Jianhui Wang, Meng Yue, Tianqiao Zhao
First: 2026-10-14T04:28:41+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T04:28:41+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
The rapid growth of AI data centers introduces large, workload-dependent loads whose protection and reconnection behavior can affect power-system transient stability. Calibrating dynamic models of such loads is challenging because treating all associated elements as freely estimable may lead to weakly identified or physically confounded parameterizations. This paper proposes an identifiability-guided calibration framework for a composite load model with separate IT, cooling, and auxiliary branches. A nuisance-projected Fisher-information criterion characterizes the local identifiability of the cooling-motor composition, while a workload non-equivalence result shows that voltage-only recalibration cannot reproduce workload-dependent responses. Guided by these results, a grey-box estimator trained with branch-level supervision learns the workload-to-power relation and the cooling composition, while retaining a prescribed UPS protection law and an externally anchored reconnect-ramp duration. The learned workload relation is then distilled into a closed form for transient-stability simulation. Evaluation using 300,000 MIT Supercloud GPU power-utilization samples and 12,600 IEEE 39-bus fault scenarios yields an aggregate active-power $R^2$ of 0.948, compared with 0.856 for an LSTM baseline. Removing branch-level supervision preserves relatively high aggregate accuracy but substantially degrades cooling-component recovery. Under unseen deep voltage sags, the proposed model achieves a transient $R^2$ of 0.750, compared with 0.033 for the LSTM. Closed-loop simulations further show that cooling composition and protection behavior can affect retained-load states and voltage responses near protection thresholds.

Traffic State Prediction and Control Using a Koopman Operator Framework with Polynomial Observables
Authors: Shakib Mustavee, Arvind Singh, Shaurya Agarwal
First: 2026-10-14T02:32:34+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T02:32:34+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
In this paper, we develop a Koopman operator framework for modeling and control of traffic flow governed by the Lighthill--Whitham--Richards (LWR) conservation law. We show that the nonlinear dynamics induced by the Greenshields flow--density relation naturally motivate a dictionary of polynomial observables, while the controlled dynamics become bilinear in the lifted Koopman coordinates. A reduced-order Koopman model is identified from spatio-temporal traffic-density snapshots and used as the prediction model in a model predictive control (MPC) framework for ramp metering. The resulting Koopman-MPC is compared with two predictive control baselines that use, respectively, a fixed linearized LWR model and the nonlinear finite-volume LWR model. In a one-hour freeway experiment, the Koopman-based controller maintains the traffic density below the critical Greenshields value and closely reproduces the behavior of the nonlinear PDE-based MPC, with a maximum-density difference below 1%. The Koopman predictor also reduces the mean online model-evaluation cost by approximately one order of magnitude relative to the nonlinear PDE predictor. In contrast, the linearized LWR predictor is computationally cheaper but exhibits substantially larger prediction errors and yields a more conservative metering policy. These results demonstrate that the analytical structure of the nonlinear traffic conservation law can be exploited to construct an interpretable data-driven surrogate for efficient traffic prediction and control

A Critical Review of Grid Integration Challenges and Flexibility Opportunities for Large-Scale Data Centers
Authors: Bibek Dhakal, Manohar Chamana, Biswambhar Gayen, Jatendar Kumar, S M Shams Rahman, Aaqib Peerzada, Ki Yeob Lee, Olatunji M. Adeyanju, Bhaskar Mitra, Soumya Kundu, Stephen Bayne
First: 2026-10-14T01:17:31+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T01:17:31+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
The rapid proliferation of cloud computing and artificial intelligence workloads is fundamentally reshaping the magnitude, spatial concentration, and electrical characteristics of data center power demand. Data centers differ from conventional commercial loads because they combine high rack densities, converter-dominated interfaces, thermally coupled cooling, uninterruptible power supplies, programmable workloads, and increasingly complex behind-the-meter generation and storage within a single facility. This review examines their grid integration from utility and sustainable-energy perspectives across resource planning, large-load interconnection, distribution feeders, market operation, real-time dynamics, power quality, and asset management. Particular attention is given to distribution-level issues that receive limited treatment in broader energy reviews: bidirectional operation on renewable-rich feeders, harmonic propagation and resonance, ride-through and reconnection, transformer derating, cable ampacity, neutral-conductor heating, protection, and measurement. Static, composite, physics-based dynamic, calibrated, impedance, electromagnetic transient, and hybrid load models are compared according to study purpose and data requirements, and utility coordination platforms, uninterruptible power supply and battery control, workload flexibility, renewable co-location, cogeneration, fuel cells, thermal storage, and clean firm supply are assessed. The central finding is that requested peak demand and annual energy matching alone cannot establish reliable or sustainable integration. Credible assessment must consider hourly and locational supply, network deliverability, marginal emissions, lifecycle and water impacts, backup obligations, rebound, asset health, and verified operating capability.

Full-Order Safety for Nonlinear Reduced-Order MPC
Authors: Luis Pabon, Johannes Köhler, Revant Kasichainula, Marco Pavone
First: 2026-10-13T22:06:52+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-13T22:06:52+00:00
Comments: 8 pages, 4 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Reduced-order models (ROMs) make model predictive control (MPC) of high-dimensional nonlinear systems tractable, but the resulting modeling error can lead to constraint violations on the real system. We introduce CoRE-MPC, a robust MPC formulation that plans with a nonlinear ROM and guarantees recursive feasibility and constraint satisfaction for the full-order system. It can be applied with a broad class of nonlinear model reduction techniques and the resulting MPC does not require evaluating the full-order model online, thus ensuring computational efficiency. The key insight is in how the ROM affects the dynamics of the prediction error. We account for this with two error radii that are propagated alongside the ROM and provide a guaranteed bound on the prediction error. The approach is validated in simulation on an 80-state oscillator chain, a Burgers equation, and a 9,768-state finite-element soft robot, where a two-state ROM with data-driven bounds keeps all 1,628 nodes clear of a wall at a median solve time below 1 ms for a 20 ms control period. Across these examples, CoRE-MPC is 13 to 61 times faster than full-order robust MPC at only 27 to 35% higher closed-loop cost, whereas existing certified schemes are either infeasible or at least twice as conservative. Dynamic Attack Surfaces: A Queue of Branching Processes
Authors: Ismail Cosandal, Abdullah Y. Etcibasi, C. Emre Koksal, Sennur Ulukus
First: 2026-10-13T17:02:46+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-13T17:02:46+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Modern attack surfaces are inherently dynamic. Vulnerabilities emerge over time and they remain exposed to exploit until defenders discover and patch them. Artificial intelligence (AI) amplifies these dynamics by accelerating both offensive and defensive processes, making modern cybersecurity increasingly a competition among interacting time scales and resources. We capture this competition using a queue of branching processes that couples vulnerability discovery and resource-constrained patching with the evolving attacker population targeting each vulnerability. Our primary risk metric is the cumulative number of exploits a vulnerability experiences before it is patched. We derive the expected cumulative number of exploits under birth-death-immigration (BDI) and rise-decay (RD) models of attacker activity. Under a BDI model of attacker activity, we identify a phase transition: the expected cumulative number of exploits is finite only when attacker growth remains below both the vulnerability discovery rate and the effective patching rate. Across both attacker models, increasing the exploit rate does not necessarily increase the expected number of exploits because faster attacker departure can sufficiently limit the active attacker population. Finally, we show that equal proportional AI amplification of offensive and defensive capabilities need not preserve security: accelerating both sides can shift the defensive bottleneck and move the system into an unbounded-risk regime.

Modern attack surfaces are inherently dynamic.
