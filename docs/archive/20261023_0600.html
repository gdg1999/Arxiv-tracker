Machine Learning-Enabled Wireless Power Transfer: A Task-Oriented Review of Structural Optimization, State and Parameter Estimation, and Adaptive Control
Authors: Denghao Luo, Yiheng Jiang, Jiaqi Zhao, Yuan Gao, Changsong Cai, Weiyi Huang, Yun Zhang
First: 2026-10-21T17:48:23+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-21T17:48:23+00:00
Comments: 42 pages, 7 figures; review article
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Wireless power transfer (WPT) systems must maintain efficient and safe energy delivery under variations in geometry, coupling, load, alignment, and operating conditions. Machine-learning methods are increasingly applied to accelerate structural design, estimate operating parameters and states, and support adaptive control and scheduling. This review develops a task-oriented framework organized around three interconnected domains: structural design and optimization, parameter and state estimation, and control and optimization. Supervised learning, reinforcement learning, and hybrid or physics-informed methods are compared in terms of data requirements, dependence on physical models, information sources, computational requirements, accuracy, online response, application scope, and optimization objectives. Supervised learning provides rapid surrogate modeling, regression, and classification when representative labeled data are available. Reinforcement learning addresses sequential control and scheduling but introduces challenges in reward design, safe exploration, and simulation-to-reality transfer. Hybrid methods integrate physical knowledge with data-driven models to improve data efficiency and physical consistency. The principal barriers to engineering deployment include limited and biased datasets, weak out-of-distribution generalization, insufficient physical consistency, unsafe online adaptation, embedded-resource constraints, and the absence of standardized benchmarks. Future research should prioritize uncertainty-aware and physics-informed learning, safe simulation-to-reality transfer, lightweight edge deployment, distributed coordination, reproducible evaluation, and digital-twin-assisted development.

Why the Swing Parameters Are Sloppy
Authors: M Parimi, Shashank Varma, Sunny Kumar, S R Wagh, Navdeep Singh
First: 2026-10-21T17:33:25+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-21T17:33:25+00:00
Comments: 23 pages, 7 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Evangelou, Stanković, Kevrekidis and Transtrum compared analytic and data-driven assessments of parameter identifiability for the infinite-bus synchronous generator. Fisher information with the manifold boundary approximation method (MBAM), and output diffusion maps with geometric harmonics, report the same six identifiable parameters on the late window $t \in [3, 5]\text{ s}$, and both place the inertia $H$ and the damping $D$ among the unidentifiable ones. We explain this result from the geometry of the dynamics rather than from that of the parameter-to-data map. The rotor swing is a fast fibre that has collapsed onto a two-dimensional normally attracting invariant manifold (NAIM) of the transient fluxes before the window opens. The zeroth-order NAIM is exactly independent of $H$, $D$, $T_{d0}''$ and $T_{q0}''$, four of the five MBAM limits. We prove, under stated smoothness and rank conditions, that each of the four limits is a boundary of the model manifold at a distance equal, to first order in the parameter, to its current profiled speed. The same analysis predicts how identifiability changes with the experiment: neither a larger initial speed deviation nor a load change restores $H$ or $D$ in the late window, whereas the early window $[0, 2]\text{ s}$ makes $H$ the best-determined parameter. Evaluated in a physical metric fixed by synchrophasor accuracy limits, the late window identifies five parameters rather than six and the early window nine. The spiked-covariance structure of the linearised trajectory map explains why the principal components of whitened, sampled trajectories reproduce the prior-scaled Fisher spectrum, and regression on those components attains the Fisher-predicted recovery error in 113 of 114 parameter--scenario pairs.

Region of Attraction Estimation for Linear Quadratic Regulator, Linear and Robust Model Predictive Control on a Two-Wheeled Inverted Pendulum
Authors: Lorenzo Fici, Dalim Wahby, Alvaro Detailleur, Matthieu Barreau, Guillaume Ducard
Venue: 2026 10th International Conference on Control, Automation and Diagnosis (ICCAD), pp. 1-6, 2026
First: 2026-04-06T06:03:18+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-21T17:18:26+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Nonlinear underactuated systems such as two-wheeled inverted pendulums (TWIPs) exhibit a limited region of attraction (RoA), which defines the set of initial conditions from which the closed-loop system converges to the equilibrium. The RoA of nonlinear and constrained systems is generally nonconvex and analytically intractable, requiring numerical or approximate estimation methods. This work investigates the estimation of the RoA for a TWIP stabilized under three model-based control strategies: saturated linear quadratic regulator (LQR), linear model predictive control (MPC), and constraint tightening MPC (CTMPC). We first derive a Lyapunov-based invariant set that provides a certified inner approximation of the RoA. Since this analytical bound is highly conservative, a Monte Carlo-based estimation procedure is then employed to obtain a more representative approximation of the RoA, capturing how the controllers behave beyond the analytically guaranteed region. The proposed methodology combines analytical guarantees with data-driven estimation, providing both a formally certified inner bound and an empirical characterization of the RoA, offering a practical way to evaluate controller performance without relying solely on conservative analytical bounds or purely empirical simulation.

Constrained Koopman Control Parametrization: Convex Controller Synthesis for Tracking under Input Constraints
Authors: Taha Ondogan, Ran Jing, Andrew P. Sabelhaus, Roberto Tron
First: 2026-10-21T16:27:42+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-21T16:27:42+00:00
Comments: 16 pages, 8 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Koopman operator theory represents nonlinear systems with linear and bilinear terms in the state and input observables, greatly simplifying control design. However, incorporating input constraints into Koopman-based controller synthesis remains challenging, limiting their applicability to constrained control tasks such as tracking. This paper develops a constrained Koopman Control Parametrization (cKoCoP) framework for synthesizing state-feedback controllers that satisfy prescribed input constraints with Lyapunov-certified stability of the identified lifted closed loop. The cKoCoP offers nonlinear state-feedback via linear-in-observables feedback, enabling linear controller design. The proposed controller is obtained through convex optimization: we incorporate ellipsoidal or polytopic input constraints with the S-procedure or Farkas-lemma-based conditions. We also introduce a Control Lyapunov Function (CLF) for trajectory tracking which incorporates input constraints. The approach is validated on two canonical nonlinear systems, a double pendulum and a unicycle, demonstrating data-driven tracking while respecting input constraints. Experiments validate the method's robustness to measurement noise, and runtime improvements over Koopman Model Predictive Control (KMPC) baselines. The results demonstrate that cKoCoP achieves competitive tracking performance with substantially reduced online computational cost, supporting its potential for real-time constrained tracking applications.

A Plantwide Simulation Library for Control-Oriented Design: Analytical and Data-driven Use Cases
Authors: Kyle Territo, Luis A. Briceno-Mena, Jose A. Romagnoli
First: 2026-10-21T16:14:05+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-21T16:14:05+00:00
Comments: 6 pages, 3 figures, Submitted to the American Control Conference (ACC) 2027
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Control structures and operability indices for plantwide processes are developed and compared on fixed simulated flowsheets, and the configuration of a study is rarely reported in a form that another group can reproduce. In this contribution, plantbench, a library of plantwide process simulations in which the design, the controller tuning and the instrumentation of a plant are declared as hashed plain-data configurations, is presented. On a heat-integrated reactor--separator--recycle plant with an open-loop unstable reactor, a heuristic plantwide control design prices four heat-exchanger networks (HENs) by the least damping ratio of the linearized closed loop, and 2016 closed-loop runs over networks, tuning, analyzer deadtime and upsets test that ranking. Results show that the damping ratio orders the networks as the runs do with the network that recovers the most heat reaching 0.013 against 0.157 for the selected one and cycling under a throughput increase, but that it is a weak detector of which operation will oscillate. At the recall of a random forest on the first 100 min after an upset, 60 of 82 limit cycles, a damping-ratio threshold raises 173 false alarms against 5. The integral time of the temperature loop, to which the damping ratio is nearly insensitive, decides in part whether the plant holds an operating point.

Worst-Case Finite-Horizon Analysis of Perturbed LQR via Performance Estimation Problem
Authors: Anthony Couthures, Gianluca Bianchin, Julien M. Hendrickx
First: 2026-10-21T15:49:58+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-21T15:49:58+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We propose an exact formulation to quantify the finite-horizon robustness of a Linear Quadratic Regulator (LQR) subject to norm-bounded Linear Time-Invariant (LTI) parametric uncertainties, using the Performance Estimation Problem (PEP) framework. Originally developed for optimization, this method allows us to reformulate the worst-case performance search as a rank-constrained Semidefinite Program (SDP). By applying operator interpolation, this formulation retains the sequence variables, allowing the worst-case to be explicitly reconstructed when a rank-one solution is found. This approach also opens new possibilities for the finite-horizon certification of broader system classes, such as data-driven models or nonlinear components. Using this framework, we observe that finite-horizon LTI perturbations do not simply follow asymptotic strategies. Instead, they align slowly decaying invariant manifolds with specific initial conditions to drive transient growth, causing a bifurcation in the worst-case behavior as the horizon length changes. Finally, we show numerically that our performance bounds sit between the upper bounds of Integral Quadratic Constraint (IQC) relaxations and a lower bound derived from a pseudospectral heuristic.

A Novel Schur Decomposition-Based Weight Projection Method for Stable State-Space Neural-Network Architectures
Authors: Sergio Vanegas, Lasse Lensu, Fredy Ruiz
First: 2026-05-14T07:28:11+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-21T11:17:20+00:00
Comments: 32 pages, 13 figures. Source code at https://codeberg.org/sergiovaneg/SchurSS
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Building black-box models for dynamical systems from data is a challenging problem in machine learning, especially when asymptotic stability guarantees are required. In this paper, we introduce a novel stability-ensuring and backpropagation-compatible projection scheme based on the Schur decomposition for the state matrix of linear discrete-time state-space layers, as well as an alternative pre-factorized formulation of the methodology. The proposed methods dynamically project the quasi-triangular factor of the state matrix's real Schur decomposition onto its nearest stable peer, ensuring stable dynamics with minimal overparameterization. Experiments on synthetic linear systems demonstrate that, despite a marginal increase in computational complexity, the method achieves accuracy and convergence rates comparable to those of state-of-the-art stable-system identification techniques. Furthermore, the lower weight count facilitates convergence during training without sacrificing accuracy in stacked neural-network architectures with static nonlinearities targeting real-world datasets. These results suggest that the Schur-based projection provides a numerically robust framework for identifying complex dynamics on par with the state of the art while satisfying strict asymptotic-stability requirements.

Integrating Flow Matching and Model Predictive Control for Safe Robot Navigation
Authors: Philipp Holzmann, Maik Pfefferkorn, Joao Carvalho, Ali Younes, An Thai Le, Georgia Chalvatzaki, Jan Peters, Rolf Findeisen
First: 2026-10-21T08:03:15+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-21T08:03:15+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Learning-based path planners based on diffusion and flow matching can generate diverse trajectories from demonstrations but typically lack mechanisms for constraint satisfaction and collision avoidance during deployment. This work proposes a framework that combines flow-matching-based path planning with model predictive path-following control to integrate data-driven path generation with real-time, constraint-aware execution. The flow-matching model captures multimodal path distributions, while the predictive controller adapts motion online to satisfy state and input constraints and avoid obstacles using updated local sensor information. An event-triggered re-planning scheme incorporates predictive control solutions into subsequent path generation, promoting collision-free navigation in environments with previously unseen obstacles. Simulation results for mobile robot navigation demonstrate robust performance across cluttered environments. The framework provides a systematic approach to combining flow matching with predictive control for adaptive, data-driven navigation.

A Decade of Learning-Based Control: From Theoretic Foundations to Practical Deployment
Authors: Angela P. Schoellig, Matthias A. Müller, Sebastian Trimpe, Melanie N. Zeilinger, Mahathi Anand, Andrea Carron, Oliver Hausdörfer, Victor G. Lopez, Friedrich Solowjow, Haocheng Zhao, Siqi Zhou
First: 2026-10-21T04:18:47+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-21T04:18:47+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Motivated by ten years of Learning-Based Control Invited Sessions at the IEEE Conference on Decision and Control (CDC), this tutorial paper reviews key advances at the intersection of machine learning and control. We focus on methods that combine the flexibility of machine learning techniques with the structure and theoretical foundations of control to design autonomous systems that are capable, reliable, and safe. We first introduce core notions for establishing stability and safety guarantees, then review approaches that learn dynamics models to improve control under uncertainty, and finally discuss data-driven methods that directly synthesize or learn policies, including reinforcement learning, imitation learning, and Bayesian optimization. We further discuss representative applications and open challenges. The goal is to provide an accessible entry point to the field and to support continued progress toward reliable learning-based control in real-world systems. To support learning and practical exploration, we share implementations and illustrative examples along with our discussion, together with additional resources on our website: https://learning-based-control.github.io/tutorial/.

Traffic State Prediction and Control Using a Koopman Operator Framework with Polynomial Observables
Authors: Shakib Mustavee, Arvind Singh, Shaurya Agarwal
First: 2026-10-14T02:32:34+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-21T03:12:36+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
In this paper, we develop a Koopman operator framework for modeling and control of traffic flow governed by the Lighthill--Whitham--Richards (LWR) conservation law. We show that the nonlinear dynamics induced by the Greenshields flow--density relation naturally motivate a dictionary of polynomial observables, while the controlled dynamics become bilinear in the lifted Koopman coordinates. A reduced-order Koopman model is identified from spatio-temporal traffic-density snapshots and used as the prediction model in a model predictive control (MPC) framework for ramp metering. The resulting Koopman-MPC is compared with two predictive control baselines that use, respectively, a fixed linearized LWR model and the nonlinear finite-volume LWR model. In a one-hour freeway experiment, the Koopman-based controller maintains the traffic density below the critical Greenshields value and closely reproduces the behavior of the nonlinear PDE-based MPC, with a maximum-density difference below 1%. The Koopman predictor also reduces the mean online model-evaluation cost by approximately one order of magnitude relative to the nonlinear PDE predictor. In contrast, the linearized LWR predictor is computationally cheaper but exhibits substantially larger prediction errors and yields a more conservative metering policy. These results demonstrate that the analytical structure of the nonlinear traffic conservation law can be exploited to construct an interpretable data-driven surrogate for efficient traffic prediction and control
