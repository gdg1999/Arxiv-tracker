On the Comparison of Reinforcement Learning and Adaptive Control for Linear Systems under Packet Loss and Uncertainty
Authors: Moh Kamalul Wafi
First: 2026-06-30T17:36:20+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-02T17:12:22+00:00
Comments: Extended and revised version of the paper published in the Proceedings of the SIAM Conference on Control and Its Applications (CT23). doi.org/10.1137/1.9781611977745
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
This paper presents a comparative study between Adaptive Quantized Control (AQC) and Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (DDPG) reinforcement learning for uncertain linear systems with input quantization over communication channels subject to packet loss. The considered setting also includes dynamic switching from a nominal unstable system to a more unstable one during operation. The AQC is designed for unknown system dynamics using acknowledgment messages to compensate for packet losses, whereas the DDPG controller is trained using the nominal system model without acknowledgment messages. Numerical results show that the DDPG controller achieves faster transient responses and improved damping within its training environment. However, under model uncertainty, packet loss, and dynamic switching, the AQC consistently demonstrates superior robustness owing to its rigorous Lyapunov stability guarantees. These results highlight the trade-off between data-driven performance and model-based robustness, and provide insight into the applicability of reinforcement learning and adaptive control for networked uncertain systems.

Algebraic Characterization of Time-Varying Set Invariance
Authors: S. B. Alaoui, E. J. Wafo Wembe, A. Saoud
First: 2026-11-02T16:00:32+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-02T16:00:32+00:00
Comments: The paper was accepted for publication in the CDC 2026
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
The verification of temporal forward invariance for dynamical systems subject to time-varying constraints is a foundational problem in the analysis of safety-critical systems. Existing methodologies rely on restrictive geometric assumptions, such as the Linear Independence Constraint Qualification (LICQ), or suffer from exponential computational complexity when applied to time-varying polytopes. This paper establishes a rigorous, unified, and computationally tractable framework for temporal safety verification. First, utilizing tools from nonsmooth analysis, we prove that a Nagumo-type geometric inclusion evaluated strictly on the boundary of the augmented graph is both necessary and sufficient for temporal forward invariance. Second, we provide an algebraic characterization of this condition requiring only the weaker Abadie Constraint Qualification (ACQ), generalizing standard Control Barrier Function (CBF) boundary conditions to non-smooth, multi-constraint domains. Finally, for the practical class of Linear Time-Invariant (LTI) systems with time-varying polytopic constraints, we deploy Farkas' Lemma to eliminate exponential vertex enumeration. By introducing a Lipschitz-bounded time-discretization scheme, we reduce the infinite-dimensional continuous-time verification problem to a finite, highly tractable set of decoupled Linear Programs (LPs).

Abstract
Accurate terminal-voltage prediction underpins model-based battery management, yet low-order equivalent-circuit models (\ecm{}) lack expressiveness under transient conditions, whereas purely data-driven predictors sacrifice interpretability and may degrade under operating-condition shift. This paper introduces a residual-corrected hybrid formulation in which a first-order Thevenin \ecm{} (\ecmrc{}) provides the dominant voltage structure, and a compact neural network embedded as a universal differential equation (\ude{}) corrects only the latent polarization mismatch. The \ecmrc{} parameters identified by nonlinear least squares warm-start the hybrid model so that the learned component operates in a low-residual regime. Experiments on a public Panasonic 18650PF dataset compare the proposed \ecmude{} with standalone \ecmrc{} and Long Short-Term Memory (\lstm{}) baselines across four axes: matched-condition prediction on UDDS at \SI{25}{\celsius}, inference-time perturbation of the supplied state-of-charge (\SOC{}, denoted $z$) input, zero-shot temperature transfer (\SI{25}{\celsius} to \SI{-20}{\celsius}), and zero-shot drive-cycle transfer to US06, LA92, and HWFET. The proposed \ecmude{} achieves the lowest voltage error in every setting, reducing mean absolute error (\mae{}) by 48\% relative to the \lstm{} under matched conditions and showing an order-of-magnitude lower inter-seed variability (coefficient of variation: 0.44\% vs.\ 6.20\%). Substantial gains persist under challenging distribution shifts, indicating that the physical model anchors prediction where a purely learned model is most vulnerable. These results position residual-corrected \ecmude{} as a lightweight and interpretable enhancement of low-order circuit models for voltage prediction in battery management systems (\bms{}).

Shadow Pricing of Static Voltage Stability Services within Unit Commitment for Inverter-Dominated Power Systems
Authors: Peng Wang, Luis Badesa
First: 2026-07-06T15:27:18+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-02T11:27:12+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Modern power systems are increasingly dominated by Inverter-Based Resources (IBR), most of which work in Grid-following (GFL) mode. This implies that they do not directly control their terminal voltage, so the static voltage stability at these buses may be compromised, especially under constant-power-factor operation that lacks voltage-adaptive reactive support. In addition, weather-driven IBR are often installed in electrically remote areas with low Short-Circuit Ratio (SCR), further exacerbating voltage issues. To address this challenge, grid-forming control can be utilized to enhance low-SCR buses, while GFL-IBR could be explicitly required to provide voltage support through grid codes. As an alternative, a market mechanism could be devised that incentivizes relevant generators to proactively adjust their operating points as a service to maintain voltage stability, while the theoretical framework for such a market has not been developed. To fill this gap, this work adopts a second-order cone-based static voltage stability constraint for GFL-IBR buses within a unit commitment problem, and proposes a mechanism to assign shadow prices to this ancillary service. To determine appropriate price values under non-convex conditions, different pricing schemes are assessed. Using a modified IEEE 30-bus system, we demonstrate that both the dispatchable and restricted pricing methods can yield revenue-adequate service prices, though the former may deliver less efficient price signals and the latter may require well-defined uplift payments. This implies that, given differentiated pricing mechanisms and price signals, operators need to select a suitable pricing method in accordance with actual system conditions and market rules.

Modern power systems are increasingly dominated by Inverter-Based Resources (IBR), most of which work in Grid-following (GFL) mode.
A Universal Policy Wrapper With Guarantees
Authors: Georgiy Malaniya, Anton Bolychev, Pavel Osinenko
First: 2026-11-02T10:22:46+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-02T10:22:46+00:00
Comments: Accepted for IEEE Access (2026)
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Many control tasks require driving the system into a prescribed goal set and subsequently keeping it there. The considered setting assumes that a working controller is available that provably reaches and maintains the goal, albeit with suboptimal performance. This controller is referred to as the fallback policy. Conversely, although state-of-the-art reinforcement-learning (RL) algorithms can discover policies with impressive empirical performance, the neural networks at their core act as opaque black boxes learned through data-driven exploration and, as such, provide no theoretical mechanism to guarantee that the system will ever reach---and stay within---the prescribed goal set. This article presents CALF-Wrapper, a method that combines the fallback policy with a pretrained RL base policy and uses the critic estimate associated with the base policy to select between them during deployment. The theoretical analysis establishes that CALF-Wrapper inherits the goal-reaching property of the fallback policy under the stated assumptions and provides probability bounds for the settling time and the maximum distance from the goal set over a prescribed time interval. The experimental study evaluates CALF-Wrapper in four simulated control environments of different complexity. Comparisons include the base policy, fallback policy, and constrained reinforcement-learning methods formulated for the same objective. Sensitivity and ablation studies provide direct hyperparameter recommendations. The experiments show how CALF-Wrapper can provide effective task performance while attaining the maximum possible observed goal-reaching rate. CALF-Wrapper requires no additional dynamics model, online learning, or constrained optimization during execution.

Many control tasks require driving the system into a prescribed goal set and subsequently keeping it there.
Protection challenges and solutions on power systems towards 100% inverter-based resources and energy storage systems

Abstract
The decarbonization of the energy sector, driven by the Paris Agreement and the European Green Deal, is increasing the share of renewable energy sources (RES) in power systems. Unlike traditional systems, RES-based systems rely on power electronic converters, which creates unprecedented challenges for reliability and resilience. The future power system is expected to combine a low-level AC layer with an upper-level High Voltage Direct Current (HVDC) backbone, which requires new understanding of fault protection, stability, and interaction between AC and DC components, as well as new models and simulation tools. This is especially critical in systems moving towards 100 percent converter-based generation. Protection systems detect, locate, and isolate faults to protect assets while the rest of the system keeps operating. Traditionally, they rely on the large fault currents supplied by synchronous machines. In modern systems, converter controls limit the fault current contribution to about 120 percent of rated current, so relays that distinguish faults from normal overcurrents based on current level may operate incorrectly. In addition, inverter-based resources (IBR) may not inject enough negative and zero sequence current for directional relays to work properly, and converters hold the voltage at a fixed value only within their reach, beyond which it drops quickly. As a result, existing protection schemes may not provide the expected accuracy and reliability. This report reviews the protection challenges identified in recent academic literature for power systems with high shares of converter-based resources. It covers the specific characteristics of RES, the challenges for protection with intelligent electronic devices (IED), the challenges of converter control during faults, and the main solutions proposed in research over the last decade.

The decarbonization of the energy sector, driven by the Paris Agreement and the European Green Deal, is increasing the share of renewable energy sources (RES) in power systems.
Convex Reformulation of Minkowski-Sum Outer Approximation for Matrix Ellipsoids in Data-Driven Control
Authors: Taira Kaminaga, Hampei Sasahara
First: 2026-11-02T05:27:36+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-02T05:27:36+00:00
Comments: 8 pages, 6 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Approximating Minkowski sums of matrix ellipsoids by a single matrix ellipsoid provides a tractable description of noise bounds for controller design from noisy data. Existing linear matrix inequality (LMI) conditions can be used to compute such approximations, but directly solving the resulting optimization problems can be computationally expensive. This paper develops an efficient method for finding a minimum-volume outer matrix ellipsoid under these LMI conditions. Although the corresponding problem for ordinary vector-valued ellipsoids is convex, the objective function in the matrix-valued case is nonconvex. We find that the problem admits an equivalent convex reformulation, which resolves this difficulty. We first eliminate the matrix variables using equivalent LMI transformations and objective monotonicity, leaving one positive scalar variable per matrix ellipsoid in the sum. We then show that the objective becomes convex after a logarithmic change of variables. The resulting problem contains neither LMI constraints nor matrix variables and can therefore be solved efficiently. Numerical experiments confirm that the proposed method is substantially faster than the LMI-based method. An application to data-driven control further illustrates the tradeoff between guaranteed control performance and computation time.

Approximating Minkowski sums of matrix ellipsoids by a single matrix ellipsoid provides a tractable description of noise bounds for controller design from noisy data.
Charge-Balanced Control of Linear Systems: Controllability, Stabilizability, and Periodic Regulation
Authors: Jie Ruan, Zonglin Liu, Olaf Stursberg, Yuan Fan, Marcel van Gerven, Yuzhen Qin
First: 2026-11-01T23:31:03+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-01T23:31:03+00:00

Abstract
Electrical neuromodulation is a therapeutic approach that regulates neural activity by delivering electrical currents to brain tissue to treat brain disorders, such as epilepsy and Parkinson's disease. A critical safety requirement is charge balance, which ensures zero net injected charge over each stimulation cycle. Despite its clinical importance, the implications of charge-balanced stimulation for system dynamics and control remain poorly understood. This article investigates the controllability, stabilizability, and regulation of discrete-time linear systems under charge-balanced input constraints, motivated by applications in electrical neuromodulation. We introduce novel notions of charge-balanced controllability and stabilizability and establish necessary and sufficient conditions for both properties. We further demonstrate that nonzero steady states cannot be maintained under charge-balanced inputs, motivating the introduction of charge-balanced periodic orbits as alternative control targets. We characterize the conditions under which convergence to such orbits is achievable and develop optimal feedback controllers to stabilize the origin and track optimal charge-balanced periodic orbits. Finally, we validate the proposed control framework using a Hodgkin-Huxley neuron model, demonstrating successful suppression of tonic spiking and highlighting the potential of our approach for controlling nonlinear dynamical systems.

A Data-Driven, Gain-Scheduled Equivalent Circuit for the B-H Behavior of Power Ferrites
Authors: Wilhelm Kirchgässner
First: 2026-11-01T21:35:01+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-01T21:35:01+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
An equivalent circuit for the dynamic B-H behavior of power ferrites is identified from measured transients. It consists of classical elements of hysteresis modeling: a nonlinear inductor for the reversible curve, six viscous play branches (inductors in series with diode-bridge sliders) that form a Prandtl-Ishlinskii model, two R-L branches for the magnetic after-effect, a mean-field feedback of H as in the Jiles-Atherton model, and a measurement path. All elements are positive apart from the feedback sources, and the circuit can run in SPICE. Discretized at the sampling step, the circuit is a difference equation with 32 parameters per material, fitted by backpropagation through time. On the 15 materials of the MagNet Challenge 2, the mean sequence relative error is 7.33 % against 7.77 % for the challenge-winning recurrent network with 336 parameters, and the circuit is more accurate on 12 of the 15 materials. The accuracy can be scaled up by gain scheduling, where a small network per material sets the element values at every step from the excitation, while the hysteresis memory stays in the circuit, such that the error drops to 5.25 % with 656 parameters per material. Thus, an interpretable model is demonstrated that achieves state-of-the-art accuracy.

A Data-Driven Krasovskii-Based Approach for Safety Controller Design of Time-Delayed Uncertain Polynomial Systems
Authors: Omid Akbarzadeh, MohammadHossein Ashoori, Amy Nejati, Abolfazl Lavaei
First: 2026-01-28T06:46:52+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-01T17:18:57+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We develop a data-driven framework for the synthesis of robust Krasovskii control barrier certificates (RK-CBC) and corresponding robust safety controllers (R-SC) for discrete-time input-affine uncertain polynomial systems with (partially) unknown dynamics, while explicitly accounting for unknown-but-bounded disturbances and time-invariant delays using only observed input-state data. Although control barrier certificates have been extensively studied for safety analysis of control systems, existing work on unknown systems with time delays, particularly in the presence of disturbances, remains limited. The challenge of safety synthesis for such systems stems from two main factors: first, the system's mathematical model is unavailable; and second, the safety conditions should explicitly incorporate the effects of time delays on system evolution during the synthesis process, while remaining robust to unknown disturbances. To address these challenges, we develop a data-driven framework based on RK-CBC, extending the classical CBC formulation for delay-free systems to explicitly account for time delays by aggregating delayed components within the barrier construction. The proposed framework relies solely on input-state data collected over a finite time horizon, enabling the direct synthesis of RK-CBC and R-SC from observed trajectories without requiring an explicit system model. The synthesis is cast as a data-driven sum-of-squares (SOS) feasibility program, yielding a structured design methodology. As a result, robust safety is guaranteed in the presence of unknown disturbances and time delays as long as the closed-loop trajectories remain within the state set. The effectiveness of the proposed method is demonstrated through three case studies, including two physical systems.
